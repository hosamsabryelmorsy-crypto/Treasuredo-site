Acetate or Metal Frames: Which Lasts Longer on Vintage Sunglasses?

Both acetate and metal frames can last for decades, and many vintage pairs prove it; which lasts longer depends on how the frame was made and how it was treated. Acetate rarely breaks from use but can crack at the hinges and craze with heat or chemicals. Metal frames resist cracking but can break at solder joints and lose their plating. Thick, well-made acetate and good-quality metal both age well.

What acetate is

Cellulose acetate is a plant-based plastic cut from sheets and polished, which is why good acetate frames have deep, layered colours that run through the material. It is different from cheaper injection-moulded plastic, where colour often sits only on the surface. Acetate can be warmed and adjusted by an optician, and usually has a metal core wire inside the temples.

How acetate ages

  • Hinge cracks: the most common damage, starting at the hinge pins.
  • Whitening or haze: from age, heat or skin oils, especially on translucent colours; light haze can often be polished away.
  • Shrinkage: very old acetate and older celluloid can shrink slightly, loosening the lenses.
  • Warping: from heat, such as being left on a car dashboard.

Metal frame materials

  • Monel and nickel silver: common, easy to solder and plate.
  • Stainless steel: strong and corrosion-resistant.
  • Titanium: light and strong, used from the 1980s.
  • Gold-filled and gold-plated: gold layers over a base metal; gold-filled has a much thicker layer than plated.

How metal frames age

  • Solder joints: the bridge and end pieces can crack; this shows as a dark line.
  • Plating wear: appears first at the nose pads, temple tips and bridge.
  • Corrosion: green or white spots at hinges and pad arms, from sweat.
  • Bending: thin metal temples bend easily but can usually be straightened.

Quick checks before buying either

  1. Open and close each temple: smooth with no wobble.
  2. Look at hinges and bridge under bright light for hairline cracks.
  3. Place the frame upside down on a table: both temples should touch.
  4. On acetate, look at the edges: colour should run through the material.
  5. On metal, check solder points and the nose pad arms.

Which to choose

Choose acetate for bold shapes, rich colour and easy adjustment; see Persol and Cazal. Choose metal for a lighter, thinner frame, such as aviators from Rodenstock or Neostyle. Whichever you pick, store sunglasses in a case and away from heat; that does more for lifespan than the material.

Cleaning and care by material

  • Acetate: wash with lukewarm water and mild soap, dry with a soft cloth. Avoid acetone, alcohol and hot water, which can haze or soften the surface. A professional polish can bring back shine on dull frames.
  • Metal: clean the hinges and pad arms with a soft brush and mild soapy water to remove sweat residue, then dry well to prevent corrosion. Do not use metal polish on plated frames, as it removes the plating.
  • Both: fold the temples before putting the frame down, always lens side up, and store in a case away from heat and direct sun.

Repair options

Loose hinges, missing screws, worn nose pads and bent temples are routine repairs for an optician on both materials. Broken solder joints on metal frames can be re-soldered by specialist repairers. Cracked acetate is harder; small cracks near hinges can sometimes be stabilised, but large cracks through the front usually cannot be fixed invisibly.

Frequently asked questions

Do acetate or metal frames last longer?

Both can last decades; acetate risks hinge cracks and haze, metal risks solder cracks and plating wear.

What is acetate in sunglasses?

A plant-based plastic cut from sheet and polished, with colour that runs through the material.

Can cracked acetate be repaired?

Small hinge cracks can sometimes be repaired, but a crack usually reduces strength and value.

Why do metal frames turn green?

Sweat can corrode the base metal at hinges and pad arms, showing as green spots.
