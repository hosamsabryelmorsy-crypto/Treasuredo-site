Presentation Watches from the Arab World: What Collectors Should Know

From the 1960s onwards, governments and rulers across the Arab world ordered watches from Swiss and Japanese makers with special dials: a national crest, a ruler's portrait or signature, or a military emblem. They were given as gifts to officials, officers, guests and employees.

What you will see on the dial

  • Oman: the khanjar, the curved dagger and crossed swords of the national emblem.
  • Gulf states: national crests and armed forces emblems.
  • Iraq, Jordan and others: portraits, eagles and royal crowns.

Why they are collected

These watches were never sold in shops. Each one was made in a limited batch for a specific order, and many were worn or lost, so good examples are scarce. They also carry a piece of the region's history.

What to check

Because the emblem is where the value lies, it is also what gets faked. Look for printing that matches the quality of the rest of the dial, a correct model for the period, and signs that the dial has not been reprinted. Buy from a seller who shows clear close-up photos and accepts returns.
