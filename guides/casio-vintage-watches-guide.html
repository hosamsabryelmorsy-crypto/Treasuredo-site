Vintage Casio Watches: Which 1970s-80s Models Are Collectible and How to Check One

The vintage Casio watches collectors want most are the early digital models of the 1970s-80s: the Casiotron, calculator watches, Data Bank models, early G-Shocks and unusual analogue-digital pieces. The best way to identify any vintage Casio is the module number stamped on the case back, and the main checks are a clear LCD, working buttons and backlight, no battery corrosion and resin that has not crumbled.

Casio watch history in brief

  • Casio was founded in Tokyo in 1946 by Tadao Kashio and later joined by his brothers, first making calculators.
  • Its first watch, the Casiotron, came out in 1974 with an automatic calendar.
  • Calculator watches followed around 1980 and became a Casio signature.
  • The first G-Shock, the DW-5000C, launched in 1983, designed to survive drops.
  • The F-91W of 1989 became one of the best-selling watches ever made.

How to identify a vintage Casio: the module number

Casio stamps a module number, usually 3 or 4 digits, on the case back, often in a small box. The module tells you the movement and functions, and you can use it to find the original manual. Many different case styles share the same module, so the module plus the model name on the front identify the exact watch.

Models collectors look for

  • Casiotron: Casio's first watches, 1970s.
  • Calculator and Data Bank watches: keypads on the dial; check every key.
  • Early G-Shock: especially early square models.
  • Ana-digi models: hands plus a digital display, often with alarms and chronographs.
  • Game and novelty watches: 1980s models with games or special functions.

What to check before you buy

  1. LCD: look for dark patches, faded segments or "bleed". A missing segment can make numbers unreadable and is hard to repair.
  2. Buttons: every button should work; test each mode (time, alarm, stopwatch, calculator).
  3. Backlight: on models with a light, check it works and is even.
  4. Battery corrosion: ask for a photo inside the case back if possible. Green or white deposits mean leaking batteries; light corrosion can be cleaned, heavy corrosion kills the module.
  5. Resin parts: old resin bezels and straps crack and crumble with age. Original resin in good shape adds value; replacements are common.
  6. Case back and screws: stripped screws and scratches suggest repeated battery changes by non-professionals.

Steel vs resin

Steel-cased vintage Casios survive better than resin ones. Steel bracelet models from the 1980s are often in better condition today, while resin models need checking for cracks around the lugs.

Questions to ask the seller

  • What is the module number on the back?
  • Do all buttons and modes work, including the light?
  • Is there any battery corrosion inside?
  • Are the strap and bezel original?

Frequently asked questions

How do I identify my vintage Casio?

Look for the 3-4 digit module number stamped on the case back; with the model name on the front it identifies the watch.

What was Casio's first watch?

The Casiotron, released in 1974 with an automatic calendar.

When was the first G-Shock made?

In 1983, the DW-5000C.

Can a faded Casio LCD be fixed?

Sometimes, but it often needs a replacement display or module, which may be hard to find for older models.
