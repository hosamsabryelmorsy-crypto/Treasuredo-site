A Short History of Cat Eye Sunglasses

The cat eye is the most recognisable women's frame shape of the twentieth century. Its upswept outer corners lift the face and still look modern today.

Origins

The shape goes back to the 1930s, when American designer Altina Schinasi created the Harlequin frame as an alternative to the plain round glasses then offered to women.

The 1950s and 1960s

By the 1950s cat eyes were everywhere, decorated with rhinestones, metal inlays and carved details. In the 1960s the shape grew larger and bolder, and Audrey Hepburn's sunglasses in Breakfast at Tiffany's in 1961 made the look famous worldwide.

Choosing a vintage pair

  • 1950s frames are small. Check the width before you buy.
  • French and Italian frames of the period are often hand finished, with real metal rivets at the hinges.
  • Rhinestones should all be present. Missing stones are hard to match.

Lenses can be replaced by any optician, so the condition of the frame matters most.
