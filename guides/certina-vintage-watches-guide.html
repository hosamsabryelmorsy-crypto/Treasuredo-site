Vintage Certina DS: What "Double Security" Means and How to Buy One

Certina DS stands for "Double Security", a construction Certina introduced in 1959 to protect the movement from shocks and water: the movement sits in a shock-absorbing mount inside a reinforced, sealed case with a thick crystal. Vintage DS models are recognised by the turtle emblem on the case back and "DS" on the dial. They are tough, well made Swiss watches that still sell for far less than comparable Omegas.

Certina in brief

  • Certina was founded in 1888 in Grenchen, Switzerland, by the brothers Adolf and Alfred Kurth.
  • The company made its own movements for much of the 20th century, so a vintage Certina movement is usually signed Certina.
  • Certina is part of the Swatch Group today, and modern DS watches still use the turtle emblem.

How the DS system works

  • Suspended movement: the movement is held in an elastic mount so knocks are absorbed before they reach the balance.
  • Reinforced case and case back: thicker than a normal dress case, with gaskets for water resistance.
  • Thick crystal: stronger than a standard thin crystal.

The turtle on the case back is the symbol of this protection. If a "DS" watch has a plain case back with no turtle, ask why.

Vintage Certina lines you will see

  • DS models: sports and everyday watches from the 1960s-70s, including divers and chronographs.
  • Blue Ribbon: Certina's dress line, slimmer and usually without the DS construction.
  • Argonaut: a 1970s line with bold case shapes.

What to check

  1. Turtle case back: it should be clearly struck. A turtle worn smooth means heavy wear or polishing.
  2. Dial: crisp printing, centred logo, small and neat "Swiss Made". Bright, perfect dials on worn cases may be repainted.
  3. Movement: should be signed Certina and match the dial (automatic, date, day-date).
  4. Crown: a signed crown is a plus; generic crowns are common after service.
  5. Gaskets: 50-year-old gaskets no longer seal. Treat the watch as not water-resistant until a watchmaker replaces and tests them.
  6. Bezel (divers): faded inserts are normal; a perfect insert on a worn case is likely a replacement.

Is a vintage Certina a good buy?

Yes, if you want a sturdy Swiss automatic for daily wear. The DS construction was designed for hard use, Certina's own movements are well regarded, and prices remain moderate. As always, an original dial and a recent service matter more than small case marks.

Questions to ask before you buy

  • When was it last serviced, and was the movement fully overhauled?
  • Can you show the movement and the inside of the case back?
  • Are the crown, hands and crystal original or replaced?
  • Does it keep time and, on date models, change the date around midnight?

Sizing and wear

DS cases are thicker than dress watches of the same era because of the reinforced construction, so a DS often wears more solidly than its diameter suggests. Blue Ribbon dress models are slimmer and suit a leather strap. Check case width without crown, lug width and thickness in the listing before you buy, especially if you plan to fit an original-style bracelet.

Frequently asked questions

What does DS mean on a Certina watch?

DS stands for Double Security, a construction introduced in 1959 with a shock-mounted movement, reinforced case and thick crystal.

Why is there a turtle on the back of my Certina?

The turtle is Certina's symbol for the DS construction, representing protection like a turtle's shell.

When was Certina founded?

In 1888 in Grenchen, Switzerland, by Adolf and Alfred Kurth.

Is a vintage Certina DS waterproof?

Not until a watchmaker replaces the old gaskets and pressure-tests the case.
