How Does a Chronograph Watch Work? Pushers, Subdials and the Tachymeter Explained

A chronograph is a watch with a built-in stopwatch. The pusher at 2 o'clock starts and stops the timing, the pusher at 4 o'clock resets it, the large central seconds hand counts seconds, and the small subdials count elapsed minutes and hours. A tachymeter scale on the bezel or dial edge turns elapsed time into speed.

Pushers

  • Top pusher (2 o'clock): start and stop.
  • Bottom pusher (4 o'clock): reset all chronograph hands to zero, used only when the chronograph is stopped.
  • Mono-pusher: some chronographs do start, stop and reset with a single button, often in the crown.
  • Flyback: a flyback lets you press reset while running, instantly restarting timing from zero.

Subdials

  • Running seconds: the normal seconds of the watch, often at 9 o'clock. It moves all the time.
  • Minute counter: usually counts up to 30 or 60 minutes of chronograph time.
  • Hour counter: counts up to 12 hours on many chronographs.
  • The central chronograph hand stays at 12 until you press start.

How to read a tachymeter

  1. Start the chronograph as you pass a marker (for example a kilometre or mile post).
  2. Stop it at the next marker.
  3. Read the number on the tachymeter scale where the central hand stopped. That is your average speed in units per hour.

The formula is 3600 ÷ seconds. One kilometre in 45 seconds gives 3600 ÷ 45 = 80 km/h. The scale works only for events shorter than one minute.

Other scales

  • Telemeter: measures distance using the delay between seeing lightning or a flash and hearing the sound.
  • Pulsometer: measures heart rate by counting a set number of beats.

Famous vintage chronograph movements

  • Valjoux 72: manual-wind, used by many Swiss brands in the 1940s-70s.
  • Valjoux 7750: automatic, introduced in the 1970s and still widely used.
  • Zenith El Primero (1969): high-beat automatic chronograph.
  • Heuer Calibre 11 (1969): early automatic chronograph with the crown on the left.
  • Seiko 6139 (1969): one of the first automatic chronographs.

How to check a vintage chronograph

  1. Start, stop and reset several times. The central hand should start without a jump and return exactly to 12.
  2. Run it past a minute: the minute counter should advance cleanly by one step.
  3. Pushers should feel crisp, not mushy or stuck.
  4. Look for a service record. Chronographs cost more to service than simple watches.
  5. Check the subdial hands all reset to zero and the printing on subdials is original.

Frequently asked questions

What does the top button on a chronograph do?

It starts and stops the stopwatch function.

How do you use a tachymeter?

Time an event over a known distance in under a minute; the number where the chronograph hand stops gives the speed in units per hour.

Should I press reset while the chronograph is running?

Only on a flyback chronograph. On others, stop it first, then reset.

Can I leave a chronograph running all the time?

It is better not to, as it adds wear; use it only when timing something.
