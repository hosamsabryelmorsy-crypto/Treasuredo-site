Vintage Citizen Automatic Watches: Seven, Leopard, Bullhead and What to Check

The vintage Citizen automatics collectors want are the Citizen Seven and Crystal Seven of the 1960s, the high-beat Leopard of the 1970s, the Bullhead chronograph and the Challenge Diver. Everyday 21-jewel day-date automatics from the 1970s-80s are the easiest entry and the cheapest to keep running. Look for an original dial, a working day-date and a movement that runs well.

Models to know

  • Citizen Seven and Crystal Seven (1960s): early Citizen automatics, often with day-date and faceted crystals on Crystal Seven models.
  • Leopard (1970s): a high-beat automatic at 28,800 vibrations per hour, often with bold dials.
  • Bullhead chronograph (1970s): crowns and pushers at the top of the case, like a stopwatch; uses the 8110 automatic chronograph calibre.
  • Challenge Diver (1970s): dive watches with rotating bezels.
  • 21-jewel day-date automatics (1970s-80s): the most common vintage Citizens, often with the 8200 series calibre.

What to check before you buy

  1. Movement: should be Citizen signed with a calibre number that matches the model. A Bullhead must have the chronograph calibre, not a plain automatic.
  2. Day-date: should change around midnight and sit centred. Many Citizen day wheels are bilingual.
  3. Dial: look for crisp printing and an evenly printed logo. Some 1970s dials have textured or coloured finishes that are hard to restore well.
  4. Case and bezel: look for sharp edges; on divers, check the bezel turns and clicks.
  5. Crown: a signed Citizen crown is a plus.
  6. Hacking and hand-winding: some calibres, such as the 8200, do not hand-wind; this is normal, not a fault.

Reading the case back

Citizen case backs show a case number and movement code. They also often carry stamps such as "GN-4-S", which refer to case specifications. A case back that clearly matches the model reference adds confidence the watch is original.

Sizing

Most vintage Citizen automatics are 35-40 mm wide, with Bullhead and divers wearing larger. Check case width without crown and lug width before buying.

Is a vintage Citizen a good buy?

Yes. They are reliable, affordable and well made, and collector models like the Bullhead and Leopard are rising in interest. Choose original dials and running movements, and budget for a service if none has been done recently.

Questions to ask the seller

  • Can you show the movement and calibre number?
  • Does the day-date change correctly?
  • Is the dial original?
  • When was it last serviced?

Frequently asked questions

What is a Citizen Bullhead?

A 1970s Citizen automatic chronograph with the crown and pushers at the top of the case, using the 8110 calibre.

What is a Citizen Leopard?

A 1970s high-beat Citizen automatic running at 28,800 vibrations per hour.

Can a Citizen 8200 be hand wound?

No. The 8200 series winds only through wrist movement; this is normal.

Are vintage Citizen automatics reliable?

Yes. They are robust and most watchmakers can service them.
