How to Clean and Store Vintage Sunglasses Without Damaging Them

Clean vintage sunglasses with lukewarm water, a drop of mild dish soap and a clean microfibre cloth, and store them in a hard case away from heat. Never use alcohol, acetone, window cleaner or hot water: these can haze acetate, damage lens coatings and loosen old lenses. Most damage to vintage frames comes from heat and harsh cleaners, not from wear.

Step-by-step cleaning

  1. Rinse first: hold the sunglasses under lukewarm running water to remove dust and grit, which would scratch the lenses if wiped dry.
  2. Wash: put a drop of mild dish soap on your fingertips and gently rub both sides of the lenses, the frame, the nose pads and the hinges.
  3. Rinse again to remove all soap.
  4. Shake and dry: shake off water and dry with a clean, soft cotton or microfibre cloth.
  5. Polish lenses with a separate clean microfibre cloth.

What not to use

  • Alcohol and acetone: can haze, soften or crack acetate and celluloid, and strip coatings.
  • Window and household cleaners: often contain ammonia, which damages coatings.
  • Paper tissues and shirts: wood fibres and dust scratch lenses.
  • Hot water: can warp acetate and loosen lenses.
  • Ultrasonic cleaners: can loosen old rivets and damage some coatings and celluloid; leave this to an optician.
  • Metal polish on plated frames: removes the gold or plating.

Cleaning the hard-to-reach parts

Dirt and skin oils build up in the hinges, around the nose pads and where the lens meets the rim. Use a soft toothbrush with soapy water on these areas, then rinse and dry well. On metal frames, dry the hinges and pad arms thoroughly to prevent corrosion.

Storage

  • Hard case: protects from crushing and scratches; a soft pouch is only for travel inside a bag.
  • Away from heat: never leave sunglasses on a car dashboard or in direct sun; heat warps acetate and damages coatings.
  • Lenses up: when you put sunglasses down outside a case, fold the temples and place them lens side up.
  • Cool, dry place: for long-term storage, avoid damp areas; moisture encourages corrosion on metal parts.
  • Separate: do not stack frames on top of each other without cases.

Small maintenance you can do

  • Check hinge screws every few months; a small eyeglass screwdriver keeps them snug. Do not overtighten.
  • Replace yellowed or hardened nose pads; they are cheap and fit most metal frames.
  • Have an optician adjust bent temples; do not bend cold acetate yourself.

For more on materials, see acetate vs metal frames.

Removing haze and stubborn marks

  • Light haze on acetate: often comes from skin oils and can be removed by washing; deeper whitening needs professional polishing.
  • Sticky residue from old labels: soften with a little mild soapy water and rub gently with a fingertip; do not scrape with a blade.
  • Green spots on metal: corrosion from sweat; clean with soapy water and a soft brush, dry well, and have an optician check the joint if it looks weak.
  • Water spots on lenses: rinse and dry with microfibre; hard water can leave marks if lenses air-dry.

Frequently asked questions

Can I clean vintage sunglasses with alcohol?

No, alcohol can haze acetate and damage lens coatings; use mild soap and water.

How should I store vintage sunglasses?

In a hard case, in a cool, dry place, away from heat and direct sun.

How do I remove grime from the hinges?

Use a soft toothbrush with soapy water, then rinse and dry well.

Can heat damage vintage sunglasses?

Yes, heat can warp acetate frames and damage lens coatings.
