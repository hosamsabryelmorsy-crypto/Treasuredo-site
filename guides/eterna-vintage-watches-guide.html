Vintage Eterna-Matic Watches: The Five-Ball Logo, KonTiki and What to Check

The Eterna-Matic is the automatic watch line Eterna launched in 1948, built around a rotor that turns on a ball bearing. The five dots in Eterna's logo represent those bearing balls. Vintage Eterna-Matics, especially the KonTiki and Centenaire, are solid Swiss automatics with an important place in watch history and are still reasonably priced.

Why Eterna matters

  • Eterna was founded in 1856 in Grenchen, Switzerland, by Josef Girard and Urs Schild.
  • Its 1948 ball-bearing rotor reduced wear and became the standard way automatic rotors are mounted in many watches today.
  • ETA, now the largest Swiss movement maker, began as Eterna's movement-making division.

Models collectors look for

  • KonTiki: introduced in the late 1950s and named after the Kon-Tiki raft expedition. Sport and dive versions carry a raft emblem on the dial or case back.
  • Centenaire: a dress line named for the company's 100th anniversary in 1956.
  • Eterna-Matic dress watches: plain dials, slim cases, often with "Eterna-Matic" and the five-ball logo on the dial.

What to check

  1. Logo: the five balls should be evenly spaced in a triangle. Uneven or blurred printing suggests a repainted dial.
  2. Movement: ask for a photo. You should see Eterna signing and, on automatics, the rotor mounted on its bearing at the centre.
  3. Rotor play: a worn bearing lets the rotor wobble or scrape the case back. A scraping sound when you move the watch means a service is due.
  4. Case: look for crisp lug edges; on gold-plated cases, check lug tips and edges for brass showing.
  5. KonTiki emblem: genuine KonTikis have the raft emblem; check it matches known examples in size and position.
  6. Crystal: acrylic scratches are easy to fix; dial damage is not.

Dial condition and value

Original dials with even ageing are worth more than restored ones. Small spots are acceptable on a 60-year-old watch. Heavy water damage or a dial that looks brand new on a worn case should lower the price.

Is a vintage Eterna a good buy?

Yes. You get a Swiss automatic from the company that made the ball-bearing rotor common, with prices well below bigger names. Choose an original dial and a running movement, and plan a service if there is no recent one.

Questions to ask before you buy

  • When was it last serviced, and was the movement fully overhauled?
  • Can you show the movement and the inside of the case back?
  • Are the crown, hands and crystal original or replaced?
  • Does it keep time and, on date models, change the date around midnight?

Sizing and wear

Most 1950s-60s Eterna-Matic dress watches are around the smaller sizes typical of the era, so measure case width without crown before buying. KonTiki sports models usually wear larger and look right on a bracelet or rubber-style strap. A plain-dial Centenaire on a leather strap is the classic dress look.

Frequently asked questions

What does the Eterna logo mean?

The five dots represent the five balls of the ball bearing used in the Eterna-Matic rotor introduced in 1948.

Is ETA related to Eterna?

Yes. ETA began as Eterna's movement-making division and later became an independent movement maker.

What is an Eterna KonTiki?

A line introduced in the late 1950s, named after the Kon-Tiki expedition, with a raft emblem on dial or case back.

When was Eterna founded?

In 1856 in Grenchen, Switzerland.
