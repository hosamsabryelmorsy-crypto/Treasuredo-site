Favre-Leuba Watches: History of One of the Oldest Swiss Brands and What to Buy

Favre-Leuba is one of the oldest Swiss watch brands, with roots going back to 1737 in Le Locle. Its vintage watches are known for innovation: the Bivouac of the 1960s was a mechanical wristwatch with an altimeter and barometer, and the Twin Power movements used two mainspring barrels. Everyday models such as the Sea King and Sea Chief are affordable, well made Swiss watches.

History in brief

  • The company traces its start to Abraham Favre in Le Locle in 1737.
  • The Leuba name joined through family marriage in the 19th century.
  • In the 20th century Favre-Leuba sold widely in India and the Middle East, which is why many vintage pieces turn up there.
  • The brand was acquired by Titan of India in 2011.

Models collectors look for

  • Bivouac: a 1960s mechanical altimeter and barometer watch for mountaineers. Rare and the most collected Favre-Leuba.
  • Bathy: a 1960s diver with a mechanical depth gauge.
  • Sea King and Sea Chief: everyday automatics and manual-wind watches, often with date.
  • Twin Power: models whose movement has two mainspring barrels for longer or more even power.

What to check

  1. Dial: crisp printing, centred logo, small neat "Swiss Made". Repaints are common on watches sold in hot, humid regions where dials often suffered damage.
  2. Movement: ask for a photo. It should be Favre-Leuba signed or a calibre that matches the model. Twin Power movements show two barrels.
  3. Case: many Sea King cases are gold plated; check for brass at lug tips and edges.
  4. Date function: the date should change near midnight and sit centred in its window.
  5. Bivouac and Bathy: the instrument must work and the readings must be legible. Damaged instrument parts are hard to source, so buy these only from sellers who show them working.
  6. Crown: a signed crown is a plus; replacements are common.

Is a vintage Favre-Leuba a good buy?

Yes. Everyday models give you Swiss heritage from one of the oldest names in watchmaking at a modest price. Collectors pay much more for Bivouac and Bathy models in working order. For any model, an original dial and a serviced movement matter most.

Questions to ask before you buy

  • When was it last serviced, and was the movement fully overhauled?
  • Can you show the movement and the inside of the case back?
  • Are the crown, hands and crystal original or replaced?
  • Does it keep time and, on date models, change the date around midnight?

Sizing and wear

Sea King and Sea Chief models are mostly in the moderate sizes typical of the 1960s-70s and suit a leather strap. Bivouac models are larger because of the instrument and wear like a tool watch. Check case width without crown and lug width before buying, and note that plated cases look best when the wear is even.
