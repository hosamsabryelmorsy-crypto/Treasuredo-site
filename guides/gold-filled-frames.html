What Does 1/10 12K GF Mean on Glasses? Gold-Filled vs Gold-Plated Eyewear

"1/10 12K GF" on a pair of glasses means the frame is gold-filled: a layer of 12-carat gold, making up one-tenth of the metal's total weight, is bonded by heat and pressure to a base metal. Gold-filled frames have far more gold than gold-plated ones and wear much more slowly, which is why many American frames from the 1930s to the 1960s still look good today. "GP" means gold-plated, a thin electroplated layer.

Common markings on vintage frames

  • 1/10 12K GF: 12-carat gold, one-tenth of the weight. The most common marking on mid-century American frames.
  • 1/20 10K GF or 1/20 12K GF: one-twentieth of the weight in gold; a thinner but still substantial layer.
  • GP or "gold plated": electroplated gold, often only a few microns thick.
  • "Doublé" or "Doublé or": the French term for gold-filled, found on French frames.
  • "Gold filled" or "rolled gold": written out in full on some frames.

Markings are usually stamped on the bridge, the inside of the temples, or the end pieces next to the hinges. They are small; use a magnifier or a phone camera zoom.

How gold-filled and gold-plated frames wear

  • Gold-filled: wear shows slowly, first at the nose pads, temple tips and edges of the rims, where the base metal starts to show as a darker or pinkish tone.
  • Gold-plated: wears faster and often flakes or leaves silvery or grey patches across contact areas.
  • Both: sweat speeds up wear and can cause green corrosion where the base metal is exposed.

How to check a gold-filled frame

  1. Find the marking: look on the bridge and temples; an unmarked frame cannot be assumed to be gold-filled.
  2. Check contact points: nose pad arms, temple tips and hinge areas show wear first.
  3. Look for solder repairs: a bright or uneven spot at the bridge or end pieces may be a repair.
  4. Avoid scratch or acid tests on a frame you want to keep; they damage the gold layer.
  5. Do not rely on a magnet: many base metals used under gold are not magnetic, so a magnet tells you little.

Who made gold-filled frames

American makers produced large numbers of gold-filled frames in the mid-20th century, including aviators and browline chassis. In France, makers such as Henry Jullien are known for gold-filled frames; see our guide to French frame makers.

Care

Wipe gold-filled and gold-plated frames with a soft damp cloth and dry them; avoid metal polish, which removes gold. Replace worn nose pads so the metal pad arms do not rub your skin. A jeweller or optician can re-plate a worn frame, but for collectors an original finish with honest wear is usually preferred.

Gold-filled, plated or solid gold: quick comparison

  • Solid gold frames are rare and are hallmarked with the gold fineness, for example 14K or 18K, often with a maker's mark; they are much heavier than gold-filled frames of the same size.
  • Gold-filled frames carry a fraction and a carat, for example 1/10 12K; the fraction is the key sign.
  • Gold-plated frames are marked GP, "gold plated" or with a micron thickness, or not marked at all.

If a frame has only a carat number such as "12K" with no fraction and no GF, look closely at the full marking before assuming it is solid gold; markings are often partly worn and the fraction may simply be hard to read.

Frequently asked questions

What does 1/10 12K GF mean on glasses?

The frame is gold-filled with 12-carat gold making up one-tenth of the metal’s total weight.

Is gold-filled better than gold-plated?

Gold-filled has a much thicker gold layer and wears far more slowly than gold-plated.

What does "Doublé" mean on French glasses?

It is the French term for gold-filled.

Are gold-filled glasses worth anything?

Gold-filled frames are valued mostly for their style and quality; the gold content is small.
