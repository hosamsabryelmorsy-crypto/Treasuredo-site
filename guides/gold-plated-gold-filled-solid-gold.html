Gold Plated vs Gold Filled vs Vermeil vs Solid Gold Watches: How to Tell

A gold-plated watch has a very thin layer of gold over base metal, a gold-filled (or rolled gold, or gold capped) watch has a much thicker layer bonded under heat and pressure, vermeil is gold over sterling silver, and solid gold is gold all the way through. You can usually tell which one you have from the case back markings, and the difference makes a big change in value and in how the watch wears over time.

The four types

  • Gold plated: a thin electroplated layer, often measured in microns (for example "10 microns" or "20 microns"). Wears through fastest at edges and lugs.
  • Gold filled / rolled gold / gold capped: a thick sheet of gold mechanically bonded to base metal. Markings include "10K GF", "1/20 12K GF", "Rolled Gold" or "Gold Capped". Lasts much longer than plating.
  • Vermeil: gold over sterling silver, marked "925" or "Argent 925", as on the Must de Cartier.
  • Solid gold: marked 375 (9k), 585 (14k) or 750 (18k), sometimes with national hallmarks.

How to read the case back

  1. Look at the outside and inside of the case back for markings.
  2. "Stainless steel back" with a gold-coloured front means a plated or capped front on a steel back.
  3. "Base metal" or "Metal" means plated.
  4. Purity numbers (585, 750) or karat marks with hallmarks mean solid gold.
  5. If there are no markings, assume plated until a jeweller tests it.

How wear looks

  • Plated cases show brass or a coppery colour at lug tips, crown and case edges.
  • Gold-filled cases show wear later and usually only in small spots.
  • Vermeil shows white silver when worn.
  • Solid gold does not wear through; it just scratches and softens.

Testing gold

A magnet does not reliably tell solid gold from plating, because many base metals are not magnetic. A jeweller can test gold with acid or an electronic tester. For buying online, rely on clear photos of the markings.

What it means for value

Solid gold watches carry material value as well as collector value. Gold-filled watches are a good middle ground with long life. Plated watches are the cheapest, and their value depends heavily on how much plating remains.

Frequently asked questions

What does 10K GF mean on a watch?

It means gold filled with 10-karat gold, a thick bonded layer over base metal.

Is gold filled better than gold plated?

Yes. Gold filled has a much thicker layer and lasts longer.

What does 750 mean on a watch case?

It means 18-karat solid gold.

Can a magnet tell if a watch is solid gold?

Not reliably; ask a jeweller for an acid or electronic test.
