Gradient Lenses Explained: Why Sunglasses Are Darker at the Top

Gradient lenses are darker at the top and lighter at the bottom because bright light mostly comes from above, while the lower part of the lens is used to look down at things like a phone, a map or a car dashboard. The result is shade where you need it and clearer vision where you need that. Gradients were especially popular on 1970s and 1980s fashion sunglasses, and many vintage pairs have them.

Types of gradient

  • Single gradient: dark at the top fading to light or clear at the bottom; the most common type.
  • Double gradient: dark at the top and the bottom with a lighter band in the middle, intended to cut glare from above and from reflective surfaces like water or snow.
  • Colour gradients: one colour fading into another, such as brown to pink or grey to blue, a fashion look common in the 1970s.

Who gradient lenses suit

  • Driving: the lighter lower half keeps the dashboard easy to read.
  • City and everyday wear: easy to see faces and screens.
  • Less suited: beach, snow or water, where light also reflects from below; full-tint or polarised lenses are better there.

How to check vintage gradient lenses

  1. Match: hold the sunglasses over white paper and compare both lenses; the fade should start and end at the same height.
  2. Even fade: the transition should be smooth, without stripes or blotches.
  3. Tint loss: older tinted plastic lenses can lighten or change colour over time; compare the centre with the edges hidden by the rim.
  4. Coating damage: look at the lens surface at an angle under light for peeling or crazing.
  5. Scratches: gradient lenses show scratches clearly in the lighter lower area.

Replacing gradient lenses

If original gradient lenses are damaged, an optician can cut new gradient sun lenses to the frame shape. Ask for the gradient to be aligned with the frame, so the dark part is at the top of the lens as worn. Prescription gradient lenses are also possible.

Gradient lenses on vintage brands

You will find gradients on many 1970s and 1980s frames from fashion houses such as Christian Dior, Nina Ricci and Yves Saint Laurent, on Cazal and Carrera frames, and on many oversized 1970s sunglasses.

Gradient, polarised or photochromic?

  • Gradient: a tint that changes from top to bottom; it reduces brightness but does not remove glare reflected from flat surfaces.
  • Polarised: a filter that blocks horizontally reflected glare from water, roads and car bonnets; the tint can be solid or gradient.
  • Photochromic: lenses that darken in UV light and clear indoors; they react slowly in cars because windscreens block much of the UV.

To check whether a vintage gradient lens is also polarised, look at a phone or computer screen through it and rotate the sunglasses 90 degrees. If the screen darkens strongly at one angle, the lens is polarised. Most vintage fashion gradients are not.

Gradient tint colours and what they do

  • Grey: darkens evenly without changing colours.
  • Brown and amber: add warmth and contrast, popular for driving.
  • Pink and rose: a soft fashion tint, lighter and better for low light than for bright sun.

Frequently asked questions

Why are some sunglasses darker at the top?

Because bright light mostly comes from above; the lighter bottom keeps things like a dashboard or phone easy to see.

What is a double gradient lens?

A lens that is dark at the top and bottom with a lighter band in the middle.

Are gradient lenses good for driving?

Yes, the lighter lower half keeps the dashboard easy to read.

Can gradient lenses be replaced?

Yes, an optician can cut new gradient lenses to the frame shape, aligned with the frame.
