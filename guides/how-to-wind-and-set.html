How to Wind and Set a Vintage Watch Without Damaging It

To wind a manual vintage watch, take it off your wrist and turn the crown clockwise (away from you) about 20-40 times until you feel firm resistance, then stop. Do it once a day at about the same time. To set the time, pull the crown out and move the hands forward, and avoid setting the date between about 9 pm and 3 am.

Winding a manual watch

  1. Take the watch off; winding on the wrist puts sideways pressure on the crown stem.
  2. With the crown pushed in, turn it clockwise in smooth turns.
  3. Stop when you feel firm resistance. Do not force past it.
  4. Wind daily, ideally in the morning, so the mainspring stays near full power during the day.

Winding an automatic watch

  • Automatics wind themselves as you move. If the watch has stopped, give it 20-30 crown turns to start it, if it hand-winds.
  • Some automatics, such as certain Seiko and Citizen calibres, do not hand-wind. Shake gently in a circular motion to start them.
  • Automatics have a slipping clutch, so you cannot overwind them.

Setting the time

  1. Unscrew the crown first if it is a screw-down crown.
  2. Pull the crown to the time-setting position (often the last position).
  3. Move the hands forward, not backwards, especially near midnight, to protect the date mechanism.
  4. Push and, if needed, screw the crown back in.

Setting the date and day

On many mechanical watches the date mechanism engages between about 9 pm and 3 am. Do not use quickset during that window. Set the hands to around 6 o'clock first, adjust the date, then set the time.

Signs of a problem

  • The crown turns without resistance and never gets firm: the mainspring may be broken.
  • Grinding or crunchy feel while winding: needs service.
  • The watch stops after a few hours even when fully wound: needs service.
  • The crown pulls out too far or falls out: the stem or setting parts need repair.

Frequently asked questions

How many times should I wind a vintage watch?

Usually 20-40 turns until you feel firm resistance.

Can I overwind a manual watch?

You should stop at firm resistance; forcing past it can damage the mainspring.

Can you overwind an automatic watch?

No, automatics have a slipping clutch.

Should I wind my watch on my wrist?

It is better to take it off to avoid sideways pressure on the crown stem.
