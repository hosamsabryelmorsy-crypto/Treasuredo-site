Jump Hour Watches Explained: How They Show the Time and What to Check

A jump hour watch shows the hour as a number in a window that "jumps" instantly to the next hour when the minutes reach 60, while the minutes are shown by a hand or a rotating disc. There is no hour hand. Vintage mechanical jump hours, especially from the 1970s, are popular because they look digital but run on a traditional movement.

How a jump hour works

  • An hour disc printed with 1-12 sits under a window.
  • A spring-loaded mechanism stores energy during the hour and releases it at the full hour, so the disc moves in one quick step.
  • Minutes are read from a minute hand or a second disc in another window.

Jump hour vs similar displays

  • Jump hour: the hour jumps instantly.
  • Disc or "mechanical digital": hour and minute discs move slowly and continuously, so the hour number creeps across the window. Many 1970s "digital" mechanical watches work this way.
  • Wandering hour: the hour number travels along an arc showing the minutes.

Sellers often call all of these "jump hour". Watch a video or ask whether the hour moves instantly.

What to check before you buy

  1. The jump: the hour should change cleanly at the full hour, not stop half-way or slip.
  2. Alignment: numbers should sit centred in their windows. Misalignment means wear or poor setting.
  3. Setting: set the time forward through several hours; the discs should move smoothly. Never force a disc backwards unless the seller confirms it is allowed.
  4. Movement: many 1970s jump hours use simple movements, sometimes pin-lever. Ask for a photo and confirm it runs well.
  5. Crystal and windows: printed or shaped crystals are hard to replace; check for cracks and fading.
  6. Discs: printed numbers should be clear, not faded or peeling.

Sizing and style

1970s jump hour and disc watches often have chunky cushion or TV-shaped cases and wear larger than their width suggests. Check case width, length and lug width.

Is a vintage jump hour worth buying?

Yes, for its unusual display and 1970s design. Pay more for a true instant jump, a clean movement and original discs and crystal.

Questions to ask the seller

  • Does the hour jump instantly or move slowly?
  • Can you show a short video of the hour changing?
  • Does it run and keep time?
  • Is the crystal original and free of cracks?

Frequently asked questions

What is a jump hour watch?

A watch with no hour hand: the hour shows as a number in a window and jumps instantly to the next hour.

Is a jump hour watch digital?

No. It looks digital but is usually mechanical, with printed discs.

What is the difference between jump hour and wandering hour?

A jump hour changes instantly; a wandering hour number moves along an arc that shows the minutes.

Are 1970s jump hour watches reliable?

They can be, but many use simple movements, so check it runs well and the hour changes cleanly.
