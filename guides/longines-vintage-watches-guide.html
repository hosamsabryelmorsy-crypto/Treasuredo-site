Vintage Longines Watches: History and What to Look For

Longines was founded in Saint-Imier, Switzerland, in 1832. Its winged hourglass logo was registered in 1889 and is one of the oldest watch trademarks still in use in its original form.

Lines to know

  • Conquest (1954): sporty automatics, often with an enamel medallion on the case back.
  • Flagship (1957): elegant dress watches with a ship engraved or stamped on the back.
  • Admiral and Ultra-Chron: 1960s and 1970s automatics, the Ultra-Chron with a high-beat movement.

Why collectors like vintage Longines

For much of the twentieth century Longines made its own movements to a very high standard. Vintage pieces offer quality close to the most famous Swiss names at lower prices.

What to check

Look for a signed movement and a clean, original dial. Longines keeps historical records, and the serial number on the movement can be used to date a watch.
