How to Measure Lug Width and Choose the Right Strap for a Vintage Watch

Lug width is the distance in millimetres between the inside edges of a watch's lugs, where the strap fits. Measure it with a ruler or calipers across the gap, and buy a strap of exactly that width. Most vintage men's watches are 17-20 mm, many ladies' watches are 10-14 mm, and odd sizes like 17 and 19 mm are more common on vintage watches than on modern ones.

How to measure

  1. Remove the strap or measure at the gap with the strap on.
  2. Use calipers if you have them; a millimetre ruler works too.
  3. Measure inside edge to inside edge, not the outside of the lugs.
  4. Round to the nearest whole millimetre. If you get 18.5 mm, check again; most watches are whole sizes.

Common lug widths

  • 16-18 mm: many 1950s-60s dress watches.
  • 19 mm: common on some Swiss vintage watches.
  • 20-22 mm: sports, divers and larger 1970s watches.
  • 10-14 mm: ladies' watches.

Choosing the strap

  • Exact width: a strap 1 mm too narrow leaves gaps and stresses the spring bars; too wide will not fit or bunches up.
  • Taper: straps often narrow towards the buckle (for example 20 to 16 mm). A taper looks more elegant on dress watches.
  • Thickness: thin leather suits slim dress watches; thicker straps suit sports and divers.
  • Length: standard straps fit most wrists; check if you have a small or large wrist.
  • Style: leather for dress watches; rubber, NATO or mesh for divers; suede and textured leather for a casual vintage look.

Spring bars

Spring bars hold the strap between the lugs. Use the correct length for your lug width, and replace old ones when changing straps; worn spring bars are a common cause of lost watches. Some vintage watches have drilled lugs (holes through the side), which make strap changes easier.

Shaped and integrated cases

Some 1970s watches use integrated bracelets or special fittings. Standard straps may not fit; you may need a specific strap or adapter. Check before buying a watch without its original bracelet.

Frequently asked questions

How do I measure lug width?

Measure the gap between the inside edges of the lugs in millimetres.

What is the most common lug width?

For vintage men's watches, 18 mm and 20 mm are very common, with 17 and 19 mm also seen.

Can I use a 20 mm strap on a 19 mm watch?

It usually will not fit properly; buy the exact width.

What is a tapered strap?

A strap that narrows from the lugs towards the buckle.
