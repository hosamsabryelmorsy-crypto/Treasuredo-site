Vintage Metzler Sunglasses: How to Identify German Frames of the 1980s

Vintage Metzler sunglasses are German-made frames, best known for their 1980s metal aviators, browlines and bold plastic shapes. You identify a genuine pair by the "Metzler" name and "Germany" marking on the frame, a four-digit model number, a colour code and a size printed inside the temple.

Where the markings are

  • Inside the left temple: usually the brand name and model number, for example a four-digit number such as 7501 or 7245.
  • Inside the right temple: often the colour code and size, written as lens width and bridge in millimetres (for example 58-18 or 52□20).
  • Country marking: "Germany" or "Made in Germany" on the temple or bridge area.
  • Lenses: some pairs carry a small logo etched on a lens; many do not, so its absence alone is not a warning sign.

Typical Metzler styles collectors search for

  • Metal aviators: teardrop or squared aviators with a double bridge or a solid top bar, often in matte black, gold or gunmetal.
  • Browline frames: a plastic upper rim over a metal lower rim, a classic 1980s German look.
  • Oversized plastic frames: thick acetate fronts in black, tortoise or two-tone colours, sometimes with metal trim on the temples.
  • Gradient lenses: brown, grey or amber lenses that fade from dark at the top to light at the bottom.

How to judge condition

Metal Metzler frames should be checked closely at the solder points of the bridge and at the hinges; a broken solder joint can be repaired but affects value. On plastic frames, look for cracks spreading from the hinge pins, which is the most common damage on 40-year-old acetate. Check that the nose pads on metal frames are present and not yellowed or hardened; pads are easy to replace and should not put you off a good frame.

Plating wear is normal on 1980s metal frames. Light wear on the temple tips or bridge is honest age; heavy flaking across the front is harder to live with. On matte black frames, shiny spots usually mean the finish has worn through where fingers touch.

Metzler compared with other German frames

Metzler sits alongside other German makers of the same period such as Cazal, Rodenstock and Neostyle. Cazal is known for very sculptural designs, Rodenstock for optical heritage and refined metal work, and Neostyle for slim, fashion-led shapes. Metzler frames tend to sit between these: well made, wearable, and often more affordable than comparable Cazal pieces.

Sizing a vintage Metzler

Read the two numbers on the temple: the first is lens width, the second is bridge width, both in millimetres. A 58 mm lens with an 18 mm bridge is a typical medium-to-large aviator; 52-20 browlines suit narrower faces. If the temple length is not printed, measure from the hinge screw to the end of the tip along the outside of the temple.

General checks before you buy

  1. Hinges: open and close each temple; it should move smoothly with no wobble. Loose screws can be tightened, cracked hinge areas cannot.
  2. Frame material: look for hairline cracks, white haze or shrinkage, especially at the bridge and near the hinges.
  3. Lenses: check for scratches, peeling coatings or delamination at the edges. Sun lenses can be replaced, but original lenses add value.
  4. Size: compare lens width, bridge and temple length with a pair you already wear; a difference of 2-3 mm in lens width is noticeable.
  5. Straightness: place the frame upside down on a table; both temples should touch the surface evenly.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the model number on Metzler sunglasses?

Inside the temple arms, usually a four-digit number next to the brand name, with the colour code and size on the opposite temple.

Are Metzler sunglasses made in Germany?

Vintage Metzler frames are marked "Germany" or "Made in Germany" on the frame.

What does 58-18 mean on Metzler sunglasses?

58 is the lens width and 18 the bridge width, both in millimetres.

Can old Metzler lenses be replaced?

Yes, an optician can cut new sun lenses to the original frame shape if the originals are scratched.
