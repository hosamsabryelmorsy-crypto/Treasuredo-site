Vintage Movado Watches: Museum Dial, Kingmatic, Calendomatic and How to Buy

Vintage Movado watches fall into two worlds. Before the Museum dial, Movado was a respected Swiss maker of mechanical watches such as the Kingmatic automatic and the Calendomatic triple calendar. From the 1960s the minimalist Museum dial, with a single dot at 12, became the brand's signature, and many later Museum watches are quartz. Knowing which one you are looking at is the key to judging value.

Movado history in brief

  • The company was founded in 1881 in La Chaux-de-Fonds, Switzerland, by Achille Ditesheim.
  • The name Movado, from Esperanto for "always in motion", was adopted in 1905.
  • The Museum dial was designed by Nathan George Horwitt in 1947 and is held in the collection of the Museum of Modern Art in New York.
  • Movado joined forces with Zenith at the end of the 1960s and was acquired in 1983 by the North American Watch Company, now Movado Group.

Vintage mechanical Movados to look for

  • Kingmatic: a 1950s-60s automatic line, often with clean dials and well-finished Movado movements.
  • Calendomatic and triple-calendar models: show day, date and month, sometimes with moon phase.
  • Polyplan: an early curved wristwatch from the 1910s with a movement built on angled levels to fit the curve. Rare.
  • Ermeto: a 1920s-30s purse or travel watch that winds as its sliding covers open and close.

Museum watches: what to know

  • Early Museum watches were mechanical; many from the 1980s onward are quartz.
  • The dial should be completely plain apart from the dot, sometimes with a small Movado name at 6 or on the case back.
  • Quartz Museum watches are worth less than mechanical ones; check the case back or movement to see which you have.

What to check before you buy

  1. Movement: ask for a photo. Vintage mechanical Movados should be signed Movado and match the dial functions.
  2. Calendar functions: on triple-calendar models, day, date and month must all advance and set correctly. Repairs to calendar works are costly.
  3. Dial: crisp printing on mechanical models; on Museum dials, check the dot is even and the dial free of scratches, since a plain dial shows every mark.
  4. Case: gold-filled and plated cases show wear at lug tips and edges; check for brass.
  5. Quartz models: look for battery corrosion in the case back photo.
  6. Bracelet: many Museum watches have integrated bracelets that are hard to replace if damaged.

Questions to ask the seller

  • Is the movement mechanical or quartz?
  • Can you show the movement and case back inside?
  • Do all calendar functions work and set correctly?
  • When was it last serviced?

Frequently asked questions

When did Movado start using the Museum dial?

The Museum dial was designed by Nathan George Horwitt in 1947, and Movado adopted it in the 1960s.

What does Movado mean?

It means "always in motion" in Esperanto. The name was adopted in 1905.

Are vintage Movado Museum watches mechanical?

Early ones were mechanical; many from the 1980s onward are quartz, which are worth less.

What is a Movado Kingmatic?

A 1950s-60s Movado automatic line with Movado movements.
