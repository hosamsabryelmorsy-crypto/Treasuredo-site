Vintage Neostyle Sunglasses: How to Identify German Neostyle Frames

Vintage Neostyle sunglasses are German frames, mostly from the 1970s to the 1990s, known for slim metal designs, distinctive shapes and fine detail on the bridge and temples. A genuine pair carries the Neostyle name, a model name or number, a size and "Germany" or "West Germany" on the frame; "West Germany" means it was made before reunification in 1990.

Markings to check

  • Brand: "Neostyle" printed or engraved inside a temple, sometimes with a model-line name next to it.
  • Model and colour: a model name or number plus a colour code.
  • Size: lens width and bridge in millimetres, often with the □ symbol, for example 48□20.
  • Country: "Germany" or "West Germany".
  • Engraving: many metal Neostyle fronts have engraved patterns on the rims or bridge; crisp engraving is a sign of good condition.

Typical Neostyle shapes

  • Small oval and round metal frames: 46-50 mm lenses, often gold-tone, sold both as sunglasses and as optical frames.
  • Geometric metal frames: octagonal, hexagonal and squared-off lenses.
  • Combination frames: metal with acetate or coloured enamel inlays on the brow or temples.
  • Aviators and navigators in thin metal.

Sunglasses or optical frame?

Many vintage Neostyle frames were made for prescription lenses and are sold today with clear demo lenses or no lenses at all. Any optician can fit sun lenses or prescription lenses into a full-rim metal frame. Before buying, check whether the listing shows tinted lenses or clear ones, and whether the frame is full-rim (easy to re-lens) or rimless (lens holes must match the new lenses).

Condition points on thin metal frames

  • Plating: look at the inside of the bridge and the temple tips, where gold plating wears first. Light wear is normal; flaking across the front is not.
  • Solder joints: the bridge and the end pieces next to the hinges are soldered; a crack there shows as a dark line.
  • Rim screws: each lens is held by a small screw at the outer edge. Make sure both are present and the lenses do not rattle.
  • Nose pads: often yellowed or hardened after decades; they are cheap to replace.
  • Temple tips: plastic tips can crack or shrink; replacements exist but originals are better for collectors.

Neostyle among German frame makers

Neostyle is often bought alongside Metzler, Cazal and Rodenstock. Cazal is the boldest of these, Rodenstock the most traditional, and Neostyle tends toward lighter, finely detailed metal frames that suit smaller faces.

Fit

Many Neostyle frames are small by today's standards. A 46-48 mm lens suits narrow faces; if your current glasses are 52 mm or more, compare front widths before you buy. Measure your current frame straight across the front from hinge to hinge and compare.

General checks before you buy

  1. Hinges: open and close each temple; it should move smoothly with no wobble. Loose screws can be tightened, cracked hinge areas cannot.
  2. Frame material: look for hairline cracks, white haze or shrinkage, especially at the bridge and near the hinges.
  3. Lenses: check for scratches, peeling coatings or delamination at the edges. Sun lenses can be replaced, but original lenses add value.
  4. Size: compare lens width, bridge and temple length with a pair you already wear; a difference of 2-3 mm in lens width is noticeable.
  5. Straightness: place the frame upside down on a table; both temples should touch the surface evenly.

Frequently asked questions

Is Neostyle a German brand?

Yes. Vintage Neostyle frames are marked "Germany" or "West Germany".

What does "West Germany" mean on Neostyle glasses?

It means the frame was made before German reunification in 1990.

Can I put sun lenses in a vintage Neostyle optical frame?

Yes, an optician can fit sun or prescription lenses into a full-rim metal frame.

Are vintage Neostyle frames small?

Many are; 46-50 mm lens widths are common, which suits narrower faces.
