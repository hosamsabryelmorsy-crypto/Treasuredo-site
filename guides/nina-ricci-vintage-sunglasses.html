Vintage Nina Ricci Sunglasses: Markings, Styles and How to Check Them

Vintage Nina Ricci sunglasses are licensed frames from the Paris fashion house founded in 1932 by Nina Ricci and her son Robert. Genuine pairs are marked "Nina Ricci" or "Nina Ricci Paris" on the temple, usually with a model code and size, and many are made in France. They are known for feminine 1970s and 1980s shapes: oversized squares, octagons and soft butterfly frames, often in pink, champagne and gradient lenses.

Reading the markings

  • Brand: "Nina Ricci" or "Nina Ricci Paris", printed or engraved inside a temple, sometimes with a logo on the outside of the temple or on the lens.
  • Model code: a number, often with a letter colour code (for example a code ending in letters that identify the colour).
  • Size: lens width and bridge in millimetres; oversized Nina Ricci frames often have lenses of 56-60 mm.
  • Origin: "Made in France" or "France" on many vintage pairs.

Signature shapes and colours

  • Octagonal frames: eight-sided lenses in metal or acetate, a strong 1970s look.
  • Oversized squares and butterflies: large lenses that sit high on the face.
  • Pink, champagne and translucent acetate: soft colours that suit the house's feminine style.
  • Gradient lenses: dark at the top fading to light or clear at the bottom, often in pink, brown or grey.

Condition checks for pale and translucent frames

Light-coloured and translucent acetate shows problems that dark frames hide. Hold the frame up to a window: small cracks around the hinge pins and the bridge are easy to see this way. Yellowing in clear acetate is common with age; even yellowing across the frame is normal, patchy discoloration may mean a repair or heat damage. On metal octagonal frames, check the small screws that hold each lens; a missing screw lets the lens fall out.

Gradient lenses can lose their tint unevenly over time. Compare both lenses side by side against white paper; they should match in colour and fade.

Why fashion-house frames are worth a look

Like Christian Dior, Yves Saint Laurent, Rochas and Pierre Cardin, Nina Ricci licensed its name to eyewear makers, and the best of these frames were produced by skilled French and European workshops. When buying, judge the frame itself: the quality of the hinges, the finish of the acetate and the clarity of the lenses matter more than the name alone.

Sizing tips

Because most Nina Ricci sunglasses are oversized, measure carefully. A 58 mm lens is large; if your current sunglasses are around 52-54 mm, expect a noticeably bigger look. Bridge width decides how the frame sits on your nose: 14-16 mm suits narrow bridges, 18-20 mm wider ones.

General checks before you buy

  1. Hinges: open and close each temple; it should move smoothly with no wobble. Loose screws can be tightened, cracked hinge areas cannot.
  2. Frame material: look for hairline cracks, white haze or shrinkage, especially at the bridge and near the hinges.
  3. Lenses: check for scratches, peeling coatings or delamination at the edges. Sun lenses can be replaced, but original lenses add value.
  4. Size: compare lens width, bridge and temple length with a pair you already wear; a difference of 2-3 mm in lens width is noticeable.
  5. Straightness: place the frame upside down on a table; both temples should touch the surface evenly.

Frequently asked questions

Are vintage Nina Ricci sunglasses made in France?

Many vintage Nina Ricci frames are marked "Made in France" or "France" on the temple.

When was Nina Ricci founded?

The Paris fashion house was founded in 1932 by Nina Ricci and her son Robert.

What styles are vintage Nina Ricci sunglasses known for?

Oversized squares, octagonal and butterfly frames, often in pink, champagne and gradient lenses.

How do I find the size of my Nina Ricci sunglasses?

Look inside the temple for two numbers: lens width and bridge width in millimetres.
