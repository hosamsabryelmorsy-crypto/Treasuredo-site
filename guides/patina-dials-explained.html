Patina on a Vintage Watch Dial: Good Character or Damage? How to Tell

Patina is the natural ageing of a watch dial and its luminous paint. Even, uniform ageing, such as cream or pumpkin-coloured tritium lume or a black dial that has turned evenly brown, is usually wanted by collectors and adds value. Uneven damage, such as water spots, corrosion, flaking paint or blotches, is not patina and lowers value.

Patina collectors like

  • Aged tritium lume: tritium was used on most watches from about the 1960s to the late 1990s. It ages from white to cream, yellow or orange-brown, and no longer glows because tritium loses half its strength about every 12 years.
  • Tropical dials: some black or dark dials turned brown from sunlight over decades. An even tropical dial can be worth more than a black one.
  • Even dial toning: silver or white dials that have turned evenly to cream or champagne.

Damage that is not patina

  • Water spots and stains: dark blotches or rings, often near the edge, from moisture inside the case.
  • Flaking or bubbling: paint or lacquer lifting off the dial.
  • Corrosion: green or brown spots on metal dials, markers or hands.
  • Spider dials: fine cracks across lacquer. Some collectors like them, but most see them as damage.

How to read the lume markings

  • "T Swiss Made T" or "Swiss T" on the dial means tritium lume.
  • Dials from before about 1960 may use radium, which is still radioactive; avoid opening or scraping them.
  • Modern non-radioactive lume (such as LumiNova, used from the 1990s) stays bright and does not age to cream in the same way.

Matching patina

The hands and dial lume should have aged to the same colour. Bright white lume on hands with cream lume on the dial, or the reverse, means the hands were replaced or relumed.

What to check in photos

  1. Ask for close-up photos in daylight, not only studio light.
  2. Look along the dial edge and around the date window for water damage.
  3. Compare the colour of hands and markers.
  4. Check whether the dial printing is intact; patina should not have eaten away the text.

Frequently asked questions

Does patina increase a watch's value?

Even, natural patina often does; uneven damage like water spots lowers value.

Why does my vintage watch lume no longer glow?

Tritium loses half its strength about every 12 years, so old tritium lume no longer glows.

What is a tropical dial?

A dark dial that has faded evenly to brown from sunlight over decades.

What does T Swiss Made T mean on a dial?

It indicates the dial uses tritium lume.
