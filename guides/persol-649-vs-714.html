Persol 649 vs 714: What Is the Difference?

The Persol 649 and 714 share almost the same shape: a bold acetate frame with a keyhole bridge and the silver Supreme Arrow on the temples. The difference is that the 714 folds: it has hinges in the bridge and temples so it collapses to pocket size. The 649 was designed in 1957 for Turin tram drivers, and the 714 became famous when Steve McQueen wore it in The Thomas Crown Affair (1968).

Key differences

  • Folding: the 714 folds at the bridge and temples; the 649 does not.
  • Origin: the 649 was made to protect drivers' eyes from wind and dust; the 714 is its folding version.
  • Feel: the 649 feels more solid; the 714 is more portable.
  • Sizes: both are commonly found in a few lens widths, around 52-56 mm.

What they share

  • Keyhole bridge.
  • Supreme Arrow metal detail on the hinges and temples.
  • Thick acetate frames in classic colours such as black and Havana tortoise.

Vintage "Persol Ratti" pairs

Older pairs are often marked "Persol Ratti", from before Luxottica acquired Persol in 1995. Vintage Ratti-era pairs are popular with collectors.

What to check before you buy

  1. Folding hinges (714): should fold and lock firmly without play.
  2. Acetate: look for cracks near hinges and the bridge, shrinkage or white haze.
  3. Arrow details: should be intact and securely attached.
  4. Lenses: check for scratches and coating damage; original lenses add value.
  5. Markings: model number and size printed inside the temple.
  6. Fit: check the lens width, bridge and temple length against glasses you wear.
