Vintage Porsche Design by Carrera Sunglasses: Interchangeable Lenses and How to Identify Them

Vintage Porsche Design sunglasses from the late 1970s and 1980s were made under licence by Carrera in Austria, and are marked "Porsche Design by Carrera" with a four-digit model number starting with 56. The best known, the 5621 aviator, has lenses that can be swapped by hand without tools, so one frame could carry different tints.

How to identify a vintage pair

  • Markings: "Porsche Design" and "by Carrera" on the frame or temples, plus "Austria" or "Made in Austria".
  • Model number: four digits, often in the 56xx range (for example 5621 or 5652), with a colour code after it.
  • Lens logo: many original lenses carry a small Porsche Design mark near the edge.
  • Size: printed on the frame, usually lens width and bridge.

How the interchangeable lenses work

On the interchangeable-lens models, each lens clips into the frame and is released by a small mechanism at the bridge or rim, depending on the model. A working pair should release and lock the lens firmly with light finger pressure. When buying, ask whether the lens change has been tested, and check that each lens sits flush with no gap at the edge. A lens that rattles or pops out with no pressure means the clip is worn.

Other features collectors look for

  • Folding frames: some models fold at the bridge and temples to fit a small case.
  • Spring hinges and flat temples: typical of the engineering-led design.
  • Finishes: matte black, silver, gold-tone and two-tone metal.
  • Original case: fitted hard cases and spare lenses add to value when present.

Condition checks

  1. Mechanism: test the lens release and any folding joints; these are the parts that wear.
  2. Finish: matte black coatings can wear shiny at the bridge and temple tips.
  3. Lenses: look for scratches, coating damage and the logo; original lenses are worth more than replacements.
  4. Hinges: spring hinges should snap back when the temple is opened past its normal position.
  5. Nose pads: replaceable, but check they are present.

Porsche Design versus Carrera-branded frames

Carrera made both its own frames and the Porsche Design line, so the build quality and some materials are similar. The Porsche Design versions are identified by the double name on the frame and by their more technical design details. Carrera-only aviators such as the Vario models are a good alternative if you like the look; see our Carrera guide.

Fit

Most vintage Porsche Design aviators are medium to large. Check the lens width on the frame and compare it with a pair you already own; flat metal temples are hard to adjust, so temple length matters more than on acetate frames.

General checks before you buy

  1. Hinges: open and close each temple; it should move smoothly with no wobble. Loose screws can be tightened, cracked hinge areas cannot.
  2. Frame material: look for hairline cracks, white haze or shrinkage, especially at the bridge and near the hinges.
  3. Lenses: check for scratches, peeling coatings or delamination at the edges. Sun lenses can be replaced, but original lenses add value.
  4. Size: compare lens width, bridge and temple length with a pair you already wear; a difference of 2-3 mm in lens width is noticeable.
  5. Straightness: place the frame upside down on a table; both temples should touch the surface evenly.
