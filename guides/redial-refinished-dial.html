How to Spot a Redialed (Refinished) Watch Dial: 8 Checks Before You Buy

A redialed watch has had its original dial repainted or reprinted, usually to hide damage. You can often spot it by blurred or thick printing, wrong fonts, a logo in the wrong position, missing or wrong "Swiss Made" text, and a dial that looks far newer than the rest of the watch. Redials are worth much less than original dials, so check carefully before buying.

8 checks to spot a redial

  1. Printing sharpness: original printing has crisp edges. Redials often have slightly fuzzy, thick or uneven letters. Zoom in on photos.
  2. Fonts and spelling: compare with known originals of the same model. Wrong fonts and spelling mistakes are clear signs.
  3. Logo and text position: the logo should be centred and level; text should be evenly spaced.
  4. Swiss Made text: look for the correct wording for the era. A missing or oversized "Swiss Made", or text that runs into the minute track, is suspicious.
  5. Minute track: markers should line up exactly with the indices. Misalignment suggests reprinting.
  6. Date window: the edges should be clean; paint inside the window cut-out is a warning sign.
  7. Lume: lume plots should be even and match the hands' age. Fresh lume on an old dial is a warning.
  8. Condition mismatch: a perfect dial in a worn case with a tired movement often means a redial.

Service dials vs redials

A service dial is an original replacement dial fitted by the brand during service. It is genuine but may differ slightly from the first dial, for example with newer lume. Service dials are worth less than original dials but more than redials.

Applied markers

On dials with applied indices, look for glue marks or uneven markers. Redials sometimes reuse old markers that are re-glued at slightly wrong positions.

When a redial is acceptable

A well-done redial on an inexpensive everyday watch can be fine if the price reflects it. For collectible models, an original dial, even with patina, is almost always worth more.

Ask the seller

  • Is the dial original, service or refinished?
  • Can you send close-up photos of the dial in daylight?
  • Has the watch been restored?

Frequently asked questions

What does redial mean?

A watch dial that has been repainted or reprinted, usually to hide damage.

Is a redialed watch worth less?

Yes, usually much less than one with an original dial.

What is a service dial?

An original replacement dial fitted by the brand during service.

How can I spot a redial in photos?

Look for blurred or thick printing, wrong fonts, off-centre logos and a dial much newer-looking than the case.
