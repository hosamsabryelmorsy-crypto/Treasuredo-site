Vintage Ricoh Watches: Are They Good? Automatics, Divers and What to Check

Vintage Ricoh watches are Japanese mechanical watches, mostly from the 1960s and 1970s, and they are good-value alternatives to Seiko and Citizen. Ricoh, known for cameras and office equipment, built a watch business in the 1960s. Today vintage Ricoh automatics and divers are affordable, distinctive and serviceable, provided the dial is original and the movement runs well.

Ricoh as a watchmaker

  • Ricoh entered watchmaking in the early 1960s by taking over an existing Japanese watch company.
  • It produced watches mainly for the Japanese and Asian markets, which is why they are less known in the West.
  • Many Ricohs from the 1970s are automatics with day-date and bold case designs.
  • Ricoh later moved to quartz like the rest of the Japanese industry.

Models you will see

  • Dress automatics: often 21 jewels, with day-date and textured or coloured dials.
  • Divers: 1970s models with rotating bezels and screw-down case backs.
  • Wide-date models: some Ricohs show a large day-date display, a feature collectors like.
  • Manual-wind models: simpler, usually from the 1960s.

What to check before you buy

  1. Movement: ask for a photo. It should be Ricoh signed, clean and free of rust. The jewel count is usually printed on the dial and movement.
  2. Day-date: both day and date should change around midnight and sit centred. Japanese day wheels are often bilingual (English and Japanese or another language).
  3. Dial: crisp printing and an evenly applied logo. Repaints exist but are less common than on Swiss brands.
  4. Bezel (divers): should turn with clear clicks; faded inserts are normal, perfect inserts on worn cases are probably replacements.
  5. Case: look for crisp lug edges and, on plated cases, brass showing at edges.
  6. Crown: a signed crown is a plus.

Parts and service

Ricoh movements are robust, but parts are less common than for Seiko. A watch that runs well is worth more than one that needs parts. Most watchmakers can clean and oil a Ricoh; ask about service history.

Is a vintage Ricoh worth buying?

Yes, if you like Japanese 1970s design and want something less common than Seiko. Choose original dials and running movements, and check that the day-date works.

Questions to ask the seller

  • Can you show the movement?
  • Does the day-date change correctly?
  • When was it last serviced?
  • Is the bezel insert original (divers)?

Frequently asked questions

Is Ricoh a watch brand?

Yes. Ricoh, best known for cameras and office equipment, made watches from the early 1960s, mainly for Japan and Asia.

Are vintage Ricoh watches automatic?

Many 1970s Ricohs are automatics, often 21 jewels with day-date.

Are Ricoh watches as good as Seiko?

They are well made and good value, though parts are less common than for Seiko.

Why are Ricoh watches rare in the US?

They were sold mainly in Japan and Asia.
