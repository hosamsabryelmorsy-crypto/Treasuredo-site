Are Vintage Roamer Watches Any Good? History, Models and What to Check

Yes, vintage Roamer watches are good Swiss watches: the company made its own movements (signed MST), built sturdy water-resistant cases, and sells for much less than better-known brands. The models you will see most are the Anfibio, Searock and Stingray. Choose one with an original dial and a running, recently serviced movement.

Roamer in brief

  • Roamer was founded in 1888 by Fritz Meyer in Solothurn, Switzerland.
  • Its movements are marked MST, for Meyer & Stüdeli, the company behind the brand.
  • Roamer produced large volumes in the 1950s-70s, so parts for common calibres are still around.

Popular vintage models

  • Anfibio: a water-resistant line from the 1950s-60s; the name means amphibious.
  • Searock: sturdy everyday automatics, often with day-date.
  • Stingray: 1970s sports models with bold case shapes and coloured dials.
  • Dress models: manual-wind and automatic watches with plain dials, often in gold-plated cases.

The one-piece case: important to know

Many Anfibio and Searock models use a one-piece case with no opening case back. The movement comes out through the front after the crystal is removed. This design kept water out well, but it means:

  • sellers often cannot show the movement, so ask whether it has been opened and serviced;
  • servicing needs a watchmaker with a crystal press and experience with these cases;
  • a cracked or loose crystal also affects water resistance and should be fixed properly.

What to check

  1. Dial: crisp printing and an evenly applied logo. Repaints have thick or uneven letters.
  2. Movement: where visible, it should be signed Roamer or MST and match the dial.
  3. Date and day: on Searock and similar models, the date should change near midnight and sit centred.
  4. Gold plating: check lug tips and edges for brass showing through.
  5. Crown: signed crowns are a plus; generic ones are common.
  6. Crystal: on one-piece cases the crystal is part of the seal, so cracks matter more than usual.

Is a vintage Roamer a good buy?

For a first Swiss mechanical watch, yes. Prices are low, the build is solid, and the 1960s-70s designs are wearable today. Pay more for original dials and for one-piece models that have been properly serviced.

Questions to ask before you buy

  • When was it last serviced, and was the movement fully overhauled?
  • Can you show the movement and the inside of the case back?
  • Are the crown, hands and crystal original or replaced?
  • Does it keep time and, on date models, change the date around midnight?

Sizing and wear

Anfibio and dress Roamers are usually modest in size, while 1970s Stingray models often have larger, bolder cases. Check case width without crown and lug width in the listing. One-piece cases tend to be slightly thicker, which gives them a solid feel on the wrist.

Frequently asked questions

Are vintage Roamer watches Swiss made?

Yes. Roamer was founded in 1888 in Solothurn, Switzerland, and made its own MST movements.

What does MST mean on a Roamer movement?

MST stands for Meyer & Stüdeli, the company behind the Roamer brand.

Why does my Roamer have no case back?

Many Anfibio and Searock models use a one-piece case that opens from the front by removing the crystal.

What is a Roamer Anfibio?

A water-resistant Roamer line from the 1950s-60s, often with a one-piece case.
