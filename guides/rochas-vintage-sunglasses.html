Vintage Rochas Paris Sunglasses: How to Identify Them and What to Check

Vintage Rochas sunglasses are licensed frames from the Paris fashion house founded in 1925 by Marcel Rochas. A genuine pair is marked "Rochas" or "Rochas Paris" on the temple, usually with a model number and size, and many vintage pairs are made in France. They are known for elegant 1970s and 1980s shapes, from oversized acetate frames to fine metal designs.

Markings on a vintage Rochas frame

  • Brand: "Rochas" or "Rochas Paris" inside a temple, sometimes with a logo on the outside of the temple.
  • Model number and colour code: usually on the same temple or the opposite one.
  • Size: lens width and bridge width in millimetres.
  • Origin: "Made in France" or "France" on many vintage frames.

Styles to know

  • Oversized acetate frames: large square or rounded fronts in tortoise, black, burgundy and translucent colours.
  • Metal frames: thin gold-tone or silver-tone frames, sometimes with decorative temples.
  • Gradient lenses: common on 1970s and 1980s pairs, usually brown or grey.

How to judge a Rochas frame

As with other fashion-house eyewear, the quality depends on the maker who produced it under licence, so check the frame itself. Good signs are smooth, deeply polished acetate edges, riveted hinges and clean, legible markings. Printed markings that are partly worn are normal after decades of use; markings that look freshly applied on an otherwise worn frame deserve a closer look.

On metal Rochas frames, inspect the plating at the bridge, the inside of the temples and the temple tips, where it wears first. Check that decorative elements on the temples are firmly attached and not glued back on. On acetate frames, check for cracks at the hinges and white haze in translucent colours.

Rochas among other Paris fashion houses

Rochas belongs to the same group of Paris houses whose sunglasses collectors look for, alongside Christian Dior, Yves Saint Laurent, Nina Ricci and Pierre Cardin. Rochas frames are found less often than Dior or YSL, which makes them a good choice if you want a Paris name that fewer people wear.

Fit and sizing

Most Rochas sunglasses from the 1970s and 1980s are medium to large. Read the size marking and compare it with sunglasses you already own. If the frame is slightly loose behind the ears, temples with a metal core wire can be gently warmed and bent by an optician; never bend cold acetate, as it can crack.

General checks before you buy

  1. Hinges: open and close each temple; it should move smoothly with no wobble. Loose screws can be tightened, cracked hinge areas cannot.
  2. Frame material: look for hairline cracks, white haze or shrinkage, especially at the bridge and near the hinges.
  3. Lenses: check for scratches, peeling coatings or delamination at the edges. Sun lenses can be replaced, but original lenses add value.
  4. Size: compare lens width, bridge and temple length with a pair you already wear; a difference of 2-3 mm in lens width is noticeable.
  5. Straightness: place the frame upside down on a table; both temples should touch the surface evenly.

Frequently asked questions

When was Rochas founded?

The Paris fashion house Rochas was founded in 1925 by Marcel Rochas.

Are vintage Rochas sunglasses made in France?

Many vintage Rochas frames are marked "Made in France" or "France".

Where is the model number on Rochas sunglasses?

Inside the temple arms, together with the size and often a colour code.

Can I adjust vintage Rochas sunglasses?

Yes, an optician can gently warm and adjust acetate temples with a metal core; do not bend them cold.
