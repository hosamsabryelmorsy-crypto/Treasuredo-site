Vintage Rodenstock Sunglasses: How to Identify and Date German Frames

Vintage Rodenstock sunglasses come from one of Germany's oldest optical companies, founded in 1877, and are identified by the Rodenstock name, a model name or number, a size marking and "Germany" or "West Germany" on the frame. Pairs marked "West Germany" were made before German reunification in 1990, which helps date them.

What to look for on the frame

  • Brand name: "Rodenstock" on the inside of a temple, sometimes also on the outside or on a temple plaque.
  • Model: many vintage Rodenstock frames carry a model name rather than just a number, which makes them easier to research.
  • Size: lens width and bridge in millimetres, often with the □ symbol between them (for example 56□18), and sometimes the temple length.
  • Country: "Germany" or "West Germany"; the latter points to a pre-1990 frame.
  • Material marks: some metal frames are stamped with a gold-plating or titanium marking on the bridge or temple.

Rodenstock Exclusiv and high-end lines

Rodenstock made premium frames under the "Exclusiv" name, with finer metal work, gold plating and higher-quality finishing. These are the pieces collectors search for most. On these frames, look for crisp engraving, solid hinges and plating that wears evenly rather than flaking.

Why the lenses matter

Rodenstock was first a lens maker, so original lenses on vintage sunglasses are often glass and of good optical quality. Hold the frame at arm's length and look through each lens at a straight line such as a door frame: the line should not bend or wobble. Glass lenses feel cool to the touch and noticeably heavier than plastic. Check the edges for small chips, which are more common on glass than scratches in the middle.

Common styles

  • Metal aviators and navigators in gold or silver tones, often with a brow bar.
  • Rimless and semi-rimless frames, where the lens is held by screws or a nylon thread rather than a full rim.
  • Classic acetate frames in tortoise, black and translucent colours.

Condition points specific to Rodenstock

On rimless frames, check each screw point on the lens for hairline cracks radiating out from the hole; these often grow over time. On semi-rimless frames, make sure the nylon thread is tight and the lens does not move in its groove; threads are cheap to replace. On gold-plated frames, inspect the inside of the bridge and the temple tips, where skin contact wears plating first.

General checks before you buy

  1. Hinges: open and close each temple; it should move smoothly with no wobble. Loose screws can be tightened, cracked hinge areas cannot.
  2. Frame material: look for hairline cracks, white haze or shrinkage, especially at the bridge and near the hinges.
  3. Lenses: check for scratches, peeling coatings or delamination at the edges. Sun lenses can be replaced, but original lenses add value.
  4. Size: compare lens width, bridge and temple length with a pair you already wear; a difference of 2-3 mm in lens width is noticeable.
  5. Straightness: place the frame upside down on a table; both temples should touch the surface evenly.

Frequently asked questions

How old is Rodenstock?

Rodenstock was founded in Germany in 1877 and is one of the oldest optical companies in the country.

What does "West Germany" on Rodenstock sunglasses mean?

It means the frame was made before German reunification in 1990.

Are vintage Rodenstock lenses glass?

Many are, especially on older pairs; glass feels cooler and heavier than plastic.

What is Rodenstock Exclusiv?

A premium Rodenstock frame line with finer metal work and finishing, popular with collectors.
