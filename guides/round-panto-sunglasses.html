Round vs Panto Sunglasses: What Is the Difference and Which Suits You

Round sunglasses have fully circular lenses, while panto sunglasses have a rounded bottom and a slightly flatter, wider top, giving a softer "keyhole" or P3 look. Both shapes go back to early spectacles and returned strongly in the 1960s and 1970s; panto frames are usually easier to wear because the flatter top follows the brow line.

How to tell them apart

  • Round: the lens is the same height as its width, a true circle.
  • Panto: the lens is slightly wider than tall, with the flatter edge at the top. Lay a ruler across the top: a panto touches along a short flat section, a round lens at a single point.
  • Oval: wider than tall with no flat section; often grouped with round frames.
  • Keyhole bridge: common on panto frames, where the bridge forms an upside-down keyhole shape that rests on the sides of the nose.

Materials on vintage round and panto frames

  • Thin metal: wire-rim round frames, gold-tone or silver-tone, often with cable temples.
  • Acetate: thicker panto frames in tortoise, black and honey colours, typical of French and Italian makers.
  • Combination: metal rims with acetate brows.

Which face shapes they suit

  • Square or angular faces: round and panto frames soften strong lines.
  • Round faces: panto frames work better than true round ones, because the flatter top adds structure.
  • Narrow faces: small round frames of 44-48 mm look balanced.
  • Wider faces: choose larger panto frames, 50 mm and up, or oversized round frames.

Sizing

Vintage round frames are often small, 42-48 mm lens width. Read the size inside the temple: the first number is lens width, the second the bridge. Because round lenses are as tall as they are wide, a small round frame still covers the eye well. Bridge fit is critical on frames without nose pads: a keyhole bridge should rest on the sides of the nose without the frame sliding down.

What to check on vintage round and panto frames

  1. Lens fit: round lenses can rotate in their rims if the rim screw is loose; check the lenses stay put.
  2. Thin metal rims: look for cracks at the rim lock beside each hinge.
  3. Cable temples: should keep their curve and not be stretched straight.
  4. Acetate pantos: look for cracks at the bridge and hinges, and white haze in translucent colours.

Many brands made both shapes; see our guides to Persol, Neostyle and French-made frames for examples.

General checks before you buy

  1. Hinges: open and close each temple; it should move smoothly with no wobble. Loose screws can be tightened, cracked hinge areas cannot.
  2. Frame material: look for hairline cracks, white haze or shrinkage, especially at the bridge and near the hinges.
  3. Lenses: check for scratches, peeling coatings or delamination at the edges. Sun lenses can be replaced, but original lenses add value.
  4. Size: compare lens width, bridge and temple length with a pair you already wear; a difference of 2-3 mm in lens width is noticeable.
  5. Straightness: place the frame upside down on a table; both temples should touch the surface evenly.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between round and panto sunglasses?

Round lenses are true circles; panto lenses have a rounded bottom and a flatter, wider top.

What is a keyhole bridge?

A bridge shaped like an upside-down keyhole that rests on the sides of the nose, common on panto frames.

Do round sunglasses suit a round face?

Panto frames usually suit round faces better, because the flatter top adds structure.

Are vintage round sunglasses small?

Often yes; 42-48 mm lens widths are common.
