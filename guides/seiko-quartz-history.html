Seiko Astron 1969: The First Quartz Watch and Vintage Seiko Quartz Worth Buying

The Seiko Quartz Astron 35SQ, released on 25 December 1969 in Japan, was the world's first quartz wristwatch to go on sale. It was accurate to within seconds a month, far better than mechanical watches, and it began the quartz revolution that changed the watch industry. Vintage Seiko quartz watches from the 1970s-80s, often marked "SQ" or "Quartz", are now collected in their own right.

The 1969 Astron in brief

  • It used a quartz crystal vibrating at 8,192 Hz to keep time.
  • It had an 18-carat gold case and was sold at the price of a small car in Japan.
  • Only a small number were made, so originals are rare and expensive.
  • Seiko shared its quartz technology widely, and quartz watches soon became cheap and common worldwide.

Vintage Seiko quartz worth knowing

  • SQ models: "SQ" on the dial means Seiko Quartz. Lines such as SQ 4004 are affordable 1970s pieces.
  • King Quartz and Grand Quartz: higher-grade 1970s quartz lines with better finishing and accuracy.
  • Twin Quartz: late 1970s models using two quartz crystals for higher accuracy.
  • Superior: top-grade 1970s quartz, very accurate.
  • Digital LCD: in 1973 Seiko released one of the first six-digit LCD digital watches; 1970s-80s Seiko digitals and ana-digi models are collected too.

What to check on a vintage Seiko quartz

  1. Running: confirm it runs on a fresh battery. A quartz watch that does not run may only need cleaning, or may have a failed circuit, which is harder to replace.
  2. Battery corrosion: a leaking battery left inside can damage contacts and circuits. Ask for a movement photo.
  3. Calibre number: usually on the movement and case back (for example 4004, 4633). It identifies the model and helps find parts.
  4. Dial: look for clean, crisp printing and an original "SQ" or "Quartz" marking.
  5. Case: many 1970s Seiko quartz watches are steel with crisp facets; polishing rounds them off.
  6. LCD (digitals): check for missing segments and fading.

Why collect vintage quartz?

These watches mark the moment the watch industry changed. Higher-grade lines such as King Quartz, Grand Quartz, Twin Quartz and Superior offer finishing and accuracy that still impress, at prices well below mechanical watches of similar quality.

Questions to ask the seller

  • Does it run on a fresh battery?
  • Is there any battery corrosion inside?
  • What calibre number is on the case back and movement?
  • Is the dial original?

Frequently asked questions

What was the first quartz wristwatch?

The Seiko Quartz Astron 35SQ, released on 25 December 1969 in Japan.

What does SQ mean on a Seiko dial?

SQ stands for Seiko Quartz.

Is a vintage Seiko quartz worth fixing?

Higher-grade models like King Quartz, Grand Quartz, Twin Quartz and Superior are worth servicing; basic models depend on parts availability.

How accurate was the Seiko Astron?

It was accurate to within seconds a month, far better than mechanical watches of the time.
