Shield and Wraparound Sunglasses: What They Are and How to Buy a Vintage Pair

Shield sunglasses use one single lens that covers both eyes, while wraparound sunglasses have a strongly curved front, with one or two lenses, that bends around the sides of the face. Both became popular from the 1980s with sports and fashion, and they block wind and side light better than flat frames. When buying vintage, the key checks are lens condition, the strength of the frame where the lens is held, and fit.

How to tell them apart

  • Shield: one continuous lens with no bridge dividing it, often with a nose piece clipped or moulded into the lens.
  • Wraparound: a curved frame front; can be a shield or two separate lenses.
  • Semi-shield: a single lens with a top bar only, no lower rim.
  • Base curve: wrap lenses are more strongly curved than flat lenses; the stronger the curve, the more they wrap.

Vintage styles to know

  • 1980s sport shields: large single lenses with plastic or metal tops, often in bright colours or mirrored finishes.
  • Fashion wraps of the 1980s-90s: from fashion houses and makers such as Carrera and Safilo.
  • 1950s-60s wraparound cat-eyes: curved acetate fronts with side shields, a much earlier take on the wrap look.

What to check

  1. Lens surface: a single shield lens has a large area to scratch; look at it against a light source at an angle.
  2. Mirror and coating wear: mirror coatings peel at the edges and around the nose; this cannot be repaired, only replaced.
  3. Mounting points: where the lens is screwed or clipped to the frame, look for hairline cracks radiating out.
  4. Nose piece: on shields, the nose piece is often a separate part; check it is present and secure.
  5. Distortion: look through the lens at a straight line; a good curved lens should not bend the line noticeably in the centre.

Replacement lenses and prescriptions

Replacing a shield lens is harder than replacing a normal lens, because it must match the exact shape and curve. Two-lens wraps can usually be re-lensed with curved sun lenses by an optician. Prescription lenses in strongly curved frames need special lens design; many opticians will advise against it for strong prescriptions.

Fit

Wraps should follow the face without touching the cheeks or eyelashes. Check total front width rather than lens width, because a single lens makes the usual lens-bridge sizing meaningless. If possible, compare the front width with a pair you already own.

General checks before you buy

  1. Hinges: open and close each temple; it should move smoothly with no wobble. Loose screws can be tightened, cracked hinge areas cannot.
  2. Frame material: look for hairline cracks, white haze or shrinkage, especially at the bridge and near the hinges.
  3. Lenses: check for scratches, peeling coatings or delamination at the edges. Sun lenses can be replaced, but original lenses add value.
  4. Size: compare lens width, bridge and temple length with a pair you already wear; a difference of 2-3 mm in lens width is noticeable.
  5. Straightness: place the frame upside down on a table; both temples should touch the surface evenly.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between shield and wraparound sunglasses?

A shield has one single lens covering both eyes; a wraparound has a curved front that bends around the face, with one or two lenses.

When were shield sunglasses popular?

Shield sunglasses were especially popular from the 1980s, with sport and fashion designs.

Can a shield lens be replaced?

It is harder than a normal lens because the shape and curve must match exactly.

How do I size shield sunglasses?

Compare the total front width with a pair you already own, since there is no separate lens and bridge size.
