What Does Swiss Made Mean on a Watch? Swiss Made vs Swiss Movement Explained

"Swiss Made" on a watch means it meets Swiss legal rules: the movement is Swiss, the watch is cased in Switzerland and the final inspection happens there. Since 2017, at least 60% of the watch's manufacturing costs must also be Swiss. "Swiss Movement" means only the movement is Swiss; the case and assembly may be from elsewhere.

The rules in short

  • Swiss movement: assembled and inspected in Switzerland, with a minimum share of Swiss value.
  • Casing in Switzerland: the movement must be put into the case in Switzerland.
  • Final inspection in Switzerland.
  • Since 2017 ("Swissness" rules): at least 60% of manufacturing costs must be Swiss, and technical development must take place in Switzerland.
  • Before 2017: rules from 1971 required the movement to meet a Swiss value threshold of 50% of component value, so many vintage watches met an older, looser standard.

Markings you will see on vintage watches

  • "Swiss Made": meets the legal standard of its time.
  • "Swiss": common on older dials, often the same meaning as Swiss Made.
  • "Swiss Movement": only the movement is Swiss; the case may be from another country.
  • "T Swiss Made T" or "Swiss T<25": Swiss Made with tritium lume.
  • "Swiss Parts": parts from Switzerland, assembled elsewhere; not Swiss Made.

Why it matters when buying

  1. "Swiss Made" watches usually carry more value than "Swiss Movement" watches.
  2. Check that the dial marking matches the movement. A Swiss Made dial with a non-Swiss movement suggests a franken or fake.
  3. On the case back, many Swiss watches also show "Swiss Made" or the country of the case.
  4. Japanese watches made for export sometimes show "Japan" or "Japan Movt" instead; this is normal for them.

Is Swiss Made always better?

Not always. Many Japanese watches from Seiko, Citizen and Orient match or beat Swiss watches of similar price. Swiss Made is a legal origin label, not a guarantee of quality.

Frequently asked questions

What does Swiss Made mean on a watch?

The movement is Swiss, the watch is cased and inspected in Switzerland, and since 2017 at least 60% of manufacturing costs are Swiss.

Is Swiss Movement the same as Swiss Made?

No. Swiss Movement means only the movement is Swiss; the case and assembly may be from elsewhere.

What does T Swiss Made T mean?

Swiss Made with tritium lume on the dial.

Is Swiss Made always better than Japanese?

No. It is an origin label; many Japanese watches are equally good.
