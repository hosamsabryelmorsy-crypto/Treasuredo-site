Vintage Tissot Watches: History, Popular Models and How to Buy One

Vintage Tissot watches are Swiss-made, well built and usually priced well below Omega, their sister brand. The models collectors look for most are the Visodate, Seastar, PR 516 and the unusual Sideral. Buy one with an original dial, a signed crown and a movement that matches the model, and you get Swiss quality at an entry-level price.

History that helps you judge a Tissot

  • Tissot was founded in 1853 in Le Locle, Switzerland, by Charles-Félicien Tissot and his son Charles-Émile.
  • In 1930 Tissot joined Omega, which is why many vintage Tissots share quality standards with Omega.
  • The Tissot Antimagnetique of 1930 is known as one of the first mass-produced anti-magnetic watches.
  • Tissot is part of the Swatch Group today, so modern reissues of vintage designs exist.

Popular vintage models

  • Visodate: a 1950s-60s automatic with date, the name refers to the visible date. Often found as "Seastar Visodate".
  • Seastar: Tissot's water-resistant everyday line; a common and affordable vintage choice.
  • PR 516: a sporty 1960s line linked to motor racing, often with a perforated rally-style bracelet or strap.
  • Sideral: a late-1960s model with a fibreglass case and bold colours, very recognisable.
  • Navigator: a world-time model with city names on the dial.

What to check before you buy

  1. Dial: printing should be crisp, the Tissot logo centred and "Swiss" or "Swiss Made" small and neat. Repaints often have thick, blurred letters.
  2. Movement: ask for a photo. It should be signed Tissot or have a Tissot calibre number. "Automatic" on the dial must match an automatic movement.
  3. Crown: a Tissot-signed crown is a good sign; generic crowns are common after service and slightly lower value.
  4. Case: look for crisp lug edges. On gold-plated cases, look for brass showing at lug tips and edges.
  5. Crystal: acrylic crystals scratch easily but are cheap to replace; dial damage is not.
  6. Date: on date models, the date should change around midnight and sit centred in the window.

Plastic movements: the Idea 2001

In the early 1970s Tissot made a movement largely of plastic (Astrolon) for the Idea 2001 line. These are interesting historically but are harder to repair than metal movements, so buy one only if it runs well.

Is vintage Tissot a good buy?

Yes, for anyone who wants a Swiss mechanical watch without Omega prices. Parts for the common calibres are available, and the designs from the 1950s-70s are clean and wearable. As with any vintage watch, a recent service matters more than small cosmetic marks.

Sizing and wearing

  • Most 1950s-60s Tissot dress watches are smaller than modern watches; check the case width without crown and the lug width before buying a strap.
  • Sports models like the PR 516 and Seastar often wear larger and suit a rally or leather strap.
  • Treat any vintage Tissot as not water-resistant until gaskets are replaced and tested.

Frequently asked questions

When was Tissot founded?

In 1853 in Le Locle, Switzerland, by Charles-Félicien Tissot and his son Charles-Émile.

Are Tissot and Omega related?

Yes. Tissot joined Omega in 1930 and both are part of the Swatch Group today.

What is a Tissot Visodate?

A 1950s-60s Tissot automatic with a date display, often sold as the Seastar Visodate.

Are vintage Tissot watches good quality?

Yes. They are Swiss-made with reliable movements and usually cost much less than comparable Omegas.
