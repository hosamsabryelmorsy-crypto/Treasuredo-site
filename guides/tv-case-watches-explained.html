What Is a TV Case Watch? 1970s TV and Cushion Cases Explained

A TV case watch has a rounded-square or rectangular case and dial shaped like an old television screen. It was a defining 1970s style. A cushion case is similar: square with softly rounded sides, like a pillow. Both wear larger than round watches of the same width, so always check width and length before buying.

TV case vs cushion case

  • TV case: the dial and crystal are a rounded rectangle or square, like a CRT screen. Often wider than tall, or the reverse.
  • Cushion case: a square case with curved sides; the dial can be round or squarish.
  • Both often have integrated lugs or bracelets and mixed brushed and polished surfaces.

Brands you will see

Nearly every brand made TV and cushion cases in the 1970s, including Omega, Seiko, Rado, Zodiac, Citizen and Orient. Many are automatics with day-date.

How to measure and size one

  1. Measure case width (without crown) and length (lug tip to lug tip). TV cases often have short or no lugs, so length is close to width.
  2. A 36-38 mm TV case wears like a 40-42 mm round watch because the corners fill more wrist.
  3. Check lug width or the bracelet fitting; integrated bracelets limit strap choices.

What to check before you buy

  1. Crystal: TV and cushion crystals are shaped, often domed or faceted. Replacements are harder to find than round crystals, so cracks and deep scratches matter.
  2. Case finish: brushed tops and polished sides should be crisp. Over-polishing rounds the shape and lowers value.
  3. Bracelet: integrated bracelets stretch and lose links; check fit and length.
  4. Movement: ask for a photo and confirm the day-date works if fitted.
  5. Case back: many 1970s TV cases are one-piece or have special backs; ask how it opens.

Questions to ask the seller

  • What are the width and length without crown?
  • Is the crystal original and free of cracks?
  • Is the bracelet original and what wrist size does it fit?
  • Does the day-date work?

Frequently asked questions

What is a TV dial watch?

A watch with a rounded-square or rectangular dial and case, shaped like an old television screen, popular in the 1970s.

Do TV case watches wear big?

Yes. A 36-38 mm TV case often wears like a 40-42 mm round watch.

What is a cushion case watch?

A square case with softly rounded sides, like a pillow.

Are TV case crystals easy to replace?

Not always; shaped crystals are harder to find than round ones.
