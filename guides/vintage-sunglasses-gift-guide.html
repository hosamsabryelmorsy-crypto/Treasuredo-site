Vintage Sunglasses as a Gift: How to Choose the Right Pair

To give vintage sunglasses as a gift, start with size: secretly borrow a pair they already wear and read the numbers inside the temple, or measure across the front from hinge to hinge. Then pick a style that matches what they already wear, choose a frame in good condition with a case, and check the return policy in case the fit is wrong. A well-chosen vintage pair is unusual, personal and often better made than a new pair at the same price.

Getting the size right without asking

  1. Borrow a pair they wear: the size printed inside the temple (for example 54□18 140) gives lens width, bridge and temple length.
  2. Measure: if there are no numbers, measure the front from hinge to hinge and compare with the listing.
  3. Stay within 2-3 mm of their current lens width for a similar look.
  4. Bridge: if they have a low or narrow nose bridge, frames with adjustable nose pads fit more easily.

Choosing a style

  • Classic and safe: aviators, tortoise acetate frames and browlines suit most people.
  • For a fashion lover: 1970s oversized frames from Paris houses such as Christian Dior, Nina Ricci or Yves Saint Laurent.
  • For a design fan: bold German frames from Cazal, or technical pieces from Porsche Design.
  • For a collector: Persol Ratti frames, with markings that tell their story.

Look at what they already wear: someone who always wears small metal frames may not enjoy a big acetate pair, however beautiful.

Condition checks for a gift

  • Lenses without scratches in the centre.
  • Hinges that open smoothly, no cracks at the hinges or bridge.
  • Both lenses the same tint.
  • Frame sits straight when placed upside down on a table.
  • Clear photos of the markings inside the temples.

What makes the gift complete

  • A hard case to protect the frame; an original case adds to the story.
  • A cleaning cloth.
  • A short note on the frame's decade and maker; buyers love knowing the history.

Timing and returns

Vintage items are one of a kind, so allow time for shipping from wherever the seller is, especially before holidays. Check the return period and whether it starts on delivery; if the size is wrong, you want enough time to return or exchange. If the lenses are not to their taste, an optician can fit new sun lenses or prescription lenses in most full-rim frames; see putting new lenses in vintage sunglasses.

Gift ideas by budget and taste

  • Everyday classic: a metal aviator or a tortoise acetate frame from a known maker.
  • Statement piece: a bold 1980s frame with colourful acetate or unusual temples.
  • Story piece: a frame from the year they were born, if the date can be confirmed from the markings and style.
  • Matching set: a vintage frame paired with an original case or a vintage lighter or pen from the same period.

When the exact decade matters, ask the seller how they dated the frame; markings, materials and style can narrow a frame to a decade, but rarely to an exact year.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find someone’s sunglasses size secretly?

Borrow a pair they wear and read the size printed inside the temple, or measure the front from hinge to hinge.

Are vintage sunglasses a good gift?

Yes, they are one of a kind and often better made than new pairs at the same price.

What vintage sunglasses suit most people?

Aviators, tortoise acetate frames and browlines suit most faces.

What if the lenses are not right?

An optician can fit new sun or prescription lenses in most full-rim frames.
