Why Do Vintage Watches Look Small? Vintage Watch Sizes Explained

Vintage watches look small because men's watches from the 1940s-60s were typically 30-36 mm wide, compared with 40-44 mm for many modern watches. Sizes grew in the 1970s, when sports and TV-case watches reached 38-42 mm. A 35 mm vintage watch can still wear well, especially with a wide dial and thin bezel, so check both case width and lug-to-lug length before buying.

Typical sizes by era

  • 1940s-50s: men's watches often 30-35 mm.
  • 1960s: 34-37 mm common for men's dress and everyday watches.
  • 1970s: wider range; dress watches stayed modest while sports, cushion and TV cases reached 38-42 mm.
  • Ladies' watches: 15-25 mm through most of the vintage period.

How to measure

  1. Case width: across the case without the crown.
  2. Lug-to-lug: from the tip of the top lugs to the tip of the bottom lugs. This decides how long the watch sits on the wrist.
  3. Thickness: from crystal to case back.
  4. Lug width: for choosing straps.

What size fits your wrist

  • Measure your wrist with a tape or a piece of string.
  • As a rough guide, the lug-to-lug should not be wider than the top of your wrist.
  • Wrists around 15-17 cm often suit 33-38 mm vintage watches; larger wrists may prefer 38 mm and up.
  • Square, cushion and TV cases wear larger than round cases of the same width.

Why a small watch can look right

  • Thin bezels and large dials make a watch look bigger than its number.
  • Light-coloured dials look larger than dark dials.
  • Short lugs keep the watch sitting neatly on smaller wrists.

Check the listing

Always look for the case width without crown and the lug-to-lug in the listing. Our listings give the case size in the title, for example "36mm".

Frequently asked questions

Why are vintage watches so small?

Men's watches from the 1940s-60s were typically 30-36 mm; larger sizes became common only from the 1970s and later.

Is a 35 mm watch too small for a man?

Not necessarily; with a large dial and thin bezel, 35 mm wears well on many wrists.

What is lug-to-lug?

The distance from the top lug tips to the bottom lug tips.

Do square watches wear larger?

Yes, square, cushion and TV cases wear larger than round cases of the same width.
