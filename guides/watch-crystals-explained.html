Acrylic vs Mineral vs Sapphire Watch Crystal: How to Tell Them Apart

Most vintage watches have acrylic crystals, which are warm-looking, cheap to replace and easy to polish but scratch easily. Mineral glass is harder and scratches less but can chip, and sapphire is very hard and scratch-resistant but can shatter on a hard knock. You can usually tell them apart with a water drop, a light tap or by looking at the edge.

The three types

  • Acrylic (also called plexiglass or Hesalite): plastic, often domed. Scratches easily but rarely shatters. Light scratches can be polished out with plastic polish.
  • Mineral glass: hardened glass, common on 1970s-90s watches. More scratch-resistant than acrylic; cannot be polished easily.
  • Sapphire: synthetic sapphire, very hard (9 on the Mohs scale, below diamond). Resists scratches; can break under a sharp impact. Common on higher-end watches from the 1980s onward and on some earlier models like Rado.

How to tell which crystal you have

  1. Water drop test: a small drop of water beads up tightly on sapphire and spreads out more on acrylic and mineral.
  2. Tap test: tapping acrylic with a fingernail gives a soft, dull sound; glass and sapphire sound sharper.
  3. Look at the edge: domed acrylic often distorts the dial edge when seen from the side.
  4. Feel: acrylic feels warmer than glass.
  5. Markings: some watches state "sapphire" on the dial or case back.

What to check before buying

  • Acrylic: light scratches are fine (easy to polish); deep cracks need a new crystal, usually inexpensive.
  • Mineral: chips at the edge are common; deep scratches mean replacement.
  • Sapphire: look for chips or cracks; replacement costs more.
  • Shaped crystals: TV, cushion and faceted crystals are harder to find than round ones.
  • Crystal fit: a loose crystal lets moisture in.

Should you replace acrylic with sapphire?

On a vintage watch, keep the original type. Acrylic is part of the look of most vintage watches, and changing it can lower collector value.

Frequently asked questions

How can I tell if my watch crystal is sapphire?

Put a small drop of water on it; on sapphire it beads up tightly.

Can you polish scratches out of a watch crystal?

Yes on acrylic, using plastic polish; mineral and sapphire cannot be polished easily.

Which watch crystal is best?

Sapphire resists scratches best, acrylic resists shattering best; vintage watches usually look right with their original type.

What is Hesalite?

A type of acrylic crystal used by some brands, including Omega.
