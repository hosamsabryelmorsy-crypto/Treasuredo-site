How Often Should a Vintage Watch Be Serviced? Signs It Needs a Service

A vintage mechanical watch should usually be serviced every 5-7 years, and right away if you do not know its service history. Oils dry out over time, causing wear even if the watch still runs. Quartz watches need less: a new battery every few years, gasket checks, and an occasional cleaning.

What a full service includes

  1. Full disassembly of the movement.
  2. Cleaning all parts.
  3. Inspecting and replacing worn parts, such as the mainspring.
  4. Re-oiling with the correct lubricants.
  5. Regulating for accuracy, often on a timing machine.
  6. Replacing gaskets and checking water resistance if needed.
  7. Demagnetising if the watch has been magnetised.

Signs your watch needs service now

  • It gains or loses several minutes a day.
  • It stops overnight even when fully wound.
  • Winding feels gritty, rough or has no resistance.
  • The automatic rotor makes a grinding or scraping noise.
  • Condensation appears under the crystal.
  • The date or day does not change properly.

Why it matters

Dry oils and dirt cause metal parts to wear against each other. A watch that is serviced in time needs fewer replacement parts, which matters on vintage watches where parts can be hard to find.

What affects service cost

  • Complexity: chronographs and calendars cost more than simple time-only watches.
  • Parts: rare vintage parts cost more and take time to find.
  • Brand: brand service centres often cost more than independent watchmakers.
  • Condition: rust, water damage or broken parts add to the work.

Quartz watches

  • Change the battery before it dies, and never leave a dead battery inside for years; it can leak.
  • Check gaskets when the battery is changed.
  • Quartz movements with gears still need occasional cleaning and oiling.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a vintage watch be serviced?

Usually every 5-7 years for mechanical watches, and immediately if the history is unknown.

What happens in a watch service?

The movement is taken apart, cleaned, oiled, adjusted and checked, with worn parts and gaskets replaced.

Do quartz watches need servicing?

Less often: regular battery changes, gasket checks and occasional cleaning.

Is it bad to leave a dead battery in a watch?

Yes; it can leak and damage the movement.
