West End Watch Co. Sowar: History of the Swiss Watch Made for India and What to Check

The West End Watch Co. Sowar is a Swiss watch line made mainly for the Indian market. "Sowar" means horseman or cavalry rider, and the name reflects the brand's long association with India, where West End watches were sold for generations. A good vintage Sowar or Sowar Prima has an original dial, a working Swiss movement and a case without heavy wear through the plating.

West End Watch Co. in brief

  • The company was founded in the 1880s by Swiss watchmaker Alfred Robert, with a strong sales base in India.
  • Its watches were assembled with Swiss movements and became everyday watches for Indian professionals, officers and families.
  • "Keepsafe" was the brand's name for its shock protection, and you will often see it printed on dials.
  • Because of this history, many vintage West End watches surface in India, the Gulf and the UK.

Sowar and Sowar Prima

  • Sowar: the name used on many manual-wind and automatic models from the mid-20th century.
  • Sowar Prima: the most common vintage model you will find, typically 1960s-70s, often with date and in steel or gold-plated cases.
  • Dials range from plain silver to sunburst and coloured, sometimes with Arabic numerals or text.

What to check before you buy

  1. Dial originality: West End watches were worn daily in hot, humid climates, so many dials were repainted. Look for crisp printing, centred logo and even spacing. Blurred or thick letters suggest a repaint.
  2. Keepsafe and Swiss Made text: should be small, neat and aligned.
  3. Movement: ask for a photo. It should be a Swiss movement in clean condition, matching the dial (automatic, date, manual).
  4. Case wear: on gold-plated cases, look at lug tips, crown and case back edges for brass showing through.
  5. Case back: look for West End signing; a plain or mismatched back is a warning sign.
  6. Crystal and crown: replaced crystals are normal; a signed crown is a plus.

Fakes and "assembled" watches

Popular names in South Asian markets attract assembled watches: a printed dial with a generic movement in a non-original case. Compare the case shape, hands and dial layout with known examples. A movement photo is the quickest way to tell; generic or very low-quality movements should be avoided.

Sizing

Most vintage Sowar models are modest in size, typical of 1960s-70s dress watches. Check case width without crown and lug width before buying a strap. They suit a slim leather strap best.

Is a West End Sowar worth buying?

Yes, for its story and Swiss build at a modest price. It is a watch with real history in India. Choose an original dial and a serviced movement, and expect fair, honest wear on a watch that was made to be used every day.

Questions to ask the seller

  • Is the dial original or restored?
  • Can you show the movement and the inside of the case back?
  • When was it last serviced?
  • Does the date (if fitted) change cleanly around midnight?

Clear answers with photos usually mean an honest watch; vague answers are a reason to look elsewhere.
