About this watch

A rare mid-20th-century Swiss-made split-second (rattrapante) pocket stopwatch, signed for the London retailer Bravingtons. Rattrapante timers are among the most complex mechanical complications: two overlapping sweep hands start together, and pressing the split pusher at 11 o'clock stops one hand to record a lap or intermediate time while the other keeps running.

The white dial has crisp black Arabic numerals, a 30-minute register at 12 signed Bravingtons London, a small seconds subdial at 6 and an outer fifth-second track marked Swiss Made. Behind the hinged back is a manual-wind Swiss movement with column-wheel control, polished steel levers and serial number 132484. The nickel/steel case has a knurled crown and bow at 12.

Brand heritage

Bravingtons was a long-established London watchmaker, jeweller and silversmith founded by J. F. Bravington, whose flagship shop stood on Pentonville Road, King's Cross, from the early 20th century. Like many leading British retailers, Bravingtons sold high-quality Swiss-made watches and timers signed with its own name.

Split-second stopwatches of this kind were made in Switzerland's specialist chronograph workshops and used for sports, racing and laboratory timing.
