WWII-Era Elgin Type A-8 “Jitterbug” Navigation Stopwatch, 15 Jewels, approx. 50mm, U.S.A., 1940s

A specialist American timing instrument from the Second World War period. The black dial has large aged Arabic numerals, a fine outer timing track and a subsidiary counter at 12 o’clock; the central hand sweeps the dial in just 10 seconds, which gives the A-8 its rapid tick and its “Jitterbug” nickname.

Brand heritage

The Elgin National Watch Company was founded in Elgin, Illinois, in 1864 and became one of the largest watch manufacturers in the United States. During the Second World War the factory turned almost entirely to military production, making service watches, chronometers, aircraft clocks, fuses and timing instruments for the U.S. armed forces.

Model history

The Type A-8 was a navigation stopwatch used for precise ground-speed timing, documented under U.S. military specification No. 94-27749. Its very fast-running movement drives a 10-second centre hand while the register at 12 records the accumulated intervals. This example has an Elgin National Watch Co. U.S.A. movement signed 15 Jewels, numbered 41797970, in a Star Watch Case Company case numbered 4981955.
