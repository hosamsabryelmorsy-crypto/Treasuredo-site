About this watch

A vintage Rado NCC 101 automatic, reference 11945, from the 1970s. The sculpted gold-plated "TV-screen" case measures approximately 41mm across and frames a crisp white dial with substantial black-lined gold baton markers and matching hands. The Rado anchor emblem sits on a red disc near twelve, the RADO signature is placed left of centre, and a framed day-date window sits at three.

The stainless-steel caseback is secured by four screws and marked RADO / NCC 101 / REF. 11945. It comes on the matching broad gold-tone bracelet with articulated links, anchor markings underneath and a Rado-signed folding clasp, and with the vintage Rado presentation box shown.

Brand heritage

Rado grew out of the Schlup & Co. movement workshop founded in Lengnau, Switzerland, in 1917, and began selling watches under the Rado name in the 1950s. In 1962 it launched the DiaStar, the first scratch-resistant watch, with a case of hardmetal and a sapphire crystal, which earned the brand its reputation as the "Master of Materials".

The NCC line of the 1970s was Rado's family of bold, large-cased automatic watches, produced alongside the DiaStar and Balboa during the most adventurous period of the brand's case design.
