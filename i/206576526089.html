Vintage Camy Genève Superautomatic – Day at 12, Date at 6 – Blue Sunburst Dial – 25 Jewels – 1970s

A 1970s Camy Genève Superautomatic with a full weekday display at twelve and a date window at six. The blue sunburst dial has applied silver-tone markers, slender hands and central seconds, and is signed CAMY, GENEVE, SUPERAUTOMATIC, 25 JEWELS and INCABLOC; it has aged to a pronounced mottled patina. The stainless steel case measures approximately 37mm wide and 12mm thick, with a broad brushed surface, angular contours and a slim polished bezel. It comes on a blue textured leather strap with contrasting light stitching, approximately 18mm wide.

Brand heritage

Camy was a Geneva watch company known for reliable, well-priced Swiss mechanical watches, sold widely across Europe, Asia and the Middle East from the 1950s through the 1970s. It also holds an interesting place in Swiss watchmaking history: Raymond Weil joined the company in 1949 and later became its director, before establishing his own brand in 1976.

Model history

The Superautomatic was Camy's line of self-winding watches in the 1960s and 1970s, fitted with 25-jewel Swiss automatic movements with Incabloc shock protection. This version, with the day spelled out in full in an arc at 12 and the date at 6, uses one of the most distinctive calendar layouts of the period, set in the angular case style typical of the 1970s.
