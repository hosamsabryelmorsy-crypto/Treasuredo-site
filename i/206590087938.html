The black dial is framed by a contrasting silver-tone chapter frame, giving the "tuxedo" look collectors love, with applied markers, the Seiko 5 shield, SEIKO AUTOMATIC signature and a day-date window at 3 o'clock with an Arabic/English day wheel.

The square "TV shape" stainless steel case measures approximately 36.5 x 34mm and wears close to 37mm, on an integrated steel bracelet with Seiko-signed clasp. The caseback is marked SEIKO Water Resistant, Stainless Steel, 6309-5030. Inside is the Seiko calibre 6309 automatic.

Brand heritage

Seiko traces its origins to 1881, when Kintaro Hattori opened a watch and clock shop in Tokyo. The company made Japan's first wristwatch, the Laurel, in 1913 and the world's first quartz wristwatch, the Astron, in 1969. The Seiko 5 line, launched in 1963, stood for five qualities: automatic winding, day-date display, water resistance, a recessed crown and a durable case. The 17-jewel calibre 6309, made from 1976 into the late 1980s, is one of Seiko's most robust automatics, best known from the 6309 "Turtle" diver.
