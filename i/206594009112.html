About this watch

A 1930s Omega wristwatch from the Art Deco years, when the wristwatch was replacing the pocket watch. The elongated rectangular case is curved to follow the line of the wrist, with stepped ends and wire lugs. The dark, heavily aged dial has Arabic numerals, a railway minute track and a small seconds register at 6 o'clock.

The case measures about 23 x 37mm and is stamped inside the snap-on back "Acier Inrouillable" (rustless steel) with the Omega hallmark and case number 8576054. Inside is a shaped Omega Swiss manual-wind movement with 15 jewels, signed "Omega Swiss", movement number 7957670, which places its manufacture in the early-to-mid 1930s.

Brand heritage

Omega's story began in 1848, when Louis Brandt set up a watch assembly workshop in La Chaux-de-Fonds, Switzerland. His sons Louis-Paul and César moved the business to Biel/Bienne in 1880, and the name Omega, first used for a celebrated 1894 calibre, became the name of the company in 1903. In the 1930s Omega produced elegant shaped wristwatches with movements made to fit rectangular and tonneau cases, the style of this example.
