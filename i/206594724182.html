About this watch

A vivid snapshot of the last years of the Soviet Union. The white dial has "Перестройка" (Perestroika) printed boldly in red, a Kremlin tower emblem with radiating lines, the Победа (Pobeda) logo, gold-tone square markers and a small seconds hand.

The tonneau-shaped case has a snap back and houses the ZIM 2602 manual-wind mechanical movement with 15 jewels. It is fitted to a silver-tone expanding bracelet.

Brand heritage

The Pobeda, Russian for "Victory", was introduced in 1945 to celebrate the end of the Second World War and became one of the most widely produced Soviet wristwatches. Later production was centred at the ZIM factory in Kuibyshev (today's Samara). In the late 1980s, during Mikhail Gorbachev's reforms, Soviet factories released watches with dials celebrating "Perestroika" (restructuring).
