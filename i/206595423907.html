About this watch

The LIP Mach 2000 is one of the great icons of 1970s industrial design. This example has the instantly recognisable black asymmetric square case with an offset round dial and the signature red ball crown sitting in the upper-right corner. The black dial has a white and orange minute track, the small lip logo, "Automatic" and a date aperture at six o'clock, with a long white minute hand and a short orange hour hand.

It is powered by a 25-jewel Duromat automatic movement, its rotor signed "Duromat Twenty-Five 25 Jewels", housed under a screw-down caseback. The case measures approximately 33mm and 9mm thick, and the watch is fitted on a black ribbed rubber strap, 18mm wide. The dial is marked Made in France.

Brand heritage

LIP was founded in Besançon, the capital of French watchmaking, in 1867 by Emmanuel Lipmann, and became a symbol of French horology. LIP was an innovator, producing Europe's first electric watch in 1952 and the first French quartz watches. In 1973 the company joined forces with the industrial designer Roger Tallon, creator of the TGV train interiors, who designed the Mach 2000 with its asymmetric case and coloured spherical crowns and pushers. The Mach 2000 design is today held in the permanent collections of MoMA in New York and the Centre Pompidou in Paris.
