About this watch

Across the Middle East and North Africa, from the 1970s to the 1990s, governments ordered wristwatches bearing the portrait of the head of state. They were not retail products: they were made in small batches and handed out as official gifts to officers, civil servants, delegations and guests of the state. In Libya such watches carried the portrait of Muammar Gaddafi, who ruled the country from 1969 to 2011, and today they are collected as historical memorabilia alongside coins, medals and banknotes of the period.

This ladies example has a colour portrait of Gaddafi in a white peaked cap and dark uniform, printed on a gold-tone dial with four small gold dot markers and slim gold hands. The cushion-shaped case is two-tone, with a gold-tone bezel on an aged silver-tone case, and the watch runs on a quartz movement. It is fitted with a grey suede-style leather strap with contrast stitching. Ladies versions are seen far less often than the men's models.

Brand heritage

Portrait watches of this kind were commissioned by governments rather than sold by a watch brand, and this example carries no maker's name on the dial. Their value lies in their history: they are tangible relics of state gift-giving in Libya during the Gaddafi era, offered here as a historical collectible.
