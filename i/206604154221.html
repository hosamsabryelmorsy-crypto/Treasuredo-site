Brand heritage

Favre-Leuba traces its roots to 1737, when Abraham Favre registered as a watchmaker in Le Locle, making it one of the oldest Swiss watch names still known today. In the 20th century the Geneva-based house became famous for robust instrument watches such as the Bivouac of 1962, the first mechanical wristwatch with an altimeter and barometer, and the Bathy diver's watch with depth gauge. The brand was especially popular in the Middle East and India, where many of its watches were sold new.

The Duomatic was Favre-Leuba's self-winding calendar line of the late 1960s and 1970s, offered in round and cushion cases.
