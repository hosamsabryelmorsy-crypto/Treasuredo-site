About this watch

A mid-century Girard-Perregaux dress watch in a cushion-shaped case with slender projecting lugs. The silver-toned dial carries applied numerals at 12 and 6, applied baton markers, gold-tone hands and the raised GP emblem above the "Girard-Perregaux Gyromatic" signature.

Inside is the signed Girard-Perregaux Gyromatic automatic movement, its rotor engraved "Girard-Perregaux Gyromatic" and the movement marked "Unadjusted", a US import marking of the period.

Brand heritage

Girard-Perregaux traces its origins to 1791 and the Geneva watchmaker Jean-François Bautte. In 1854 the watchmaker Constant Girard married Marie Perregaux, and in 1856 they founded Girard-Perregaux in La Chaux-de-Fonds. The house is famous for its Tourbillon with Three Gold Bridges, which won a gold medal at the Paris Universal Exhibition of 1889, and it remains one of the Swiss manufactures that make their own movements.

The Gyromatic was introduced in 1957 and takes its name from the "Gyrotron" winding system, a compact arrangement of roller clutches that lets the rotor wind efficiently in both directions and allowed a slim automatic movement. It became the basis of GP's automatics through the 1960s.
