Brand heritage

Rado grew out of the Schlup & Co. movement workshop founded in Lengnau, Switzerland, in 1917, and began selling watches under the Rado name in the 1950s. The brand became known as the "Master of Materials" for pioneering hardmetal, sapphire crystal and, later, high-tech ceramic in watchmaking. The DiaStar, introduced in 1962, was the first scratch-resistant watch, with a case top of tungsten-carbide hardmetal and a sapphire crystal. Its shield-shaped case became the signature of the brand, and the moving anchor emblem on the dial, which rotates freely on a jewel, was Rado's mark for its automatic watches.
