About this watch

A vintage Rado DiaStar Alarm from the 1970s, combining an automatic movement with a mechanical alarm. The finely brushed silver-white dial is framed by a blue-grey ring and has applied metallic hour markers, a red-tipped alarm hand, the red Rado anchor emblem and a framed day-date display with the day in Arabic.

The angular, faceted tungsten-carbide hardmetal case measures approximately 40mm across and 15mm thick, with two crowns (time and alarm) and a screw-down stainless-steel caseback signed Rado Watch Co. The Rado-signed rotor is marked 6801 and the movement is marked 25 jewels, Swiss. It comes on a stainless steel bracelet, approximately 24mm wide, and with a Rado box.

Brand heritage

Rado grew out of the Schlup & Co. movement workshop founded in Lengnau, Switzerland, in 1917, and began selling watches under the Rado name in the 1950s. The brand became known as the "Master of Materials" for pioneering hardmetal, sapphire crystal and, later, high-tech ceramic in watchmaking. The DiaStar, introduced in 1962, was the first scratch-resistant watch, with a case of tungsten-carbide hardmetal. In the 1970s Rado added an alarm version to the line, with an automatic alarm calibre offering centre seconds, day and date and a mechanical alarm set by the second crown.
