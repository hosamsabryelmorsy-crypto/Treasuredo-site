About this watch

A 1940s Swiss chronograph attributed to Pierce. The cream dial is dominated by a bold seconds track numbered 5 to 60 in large black serif numerals, with a fine railway minute scale, dark sword hands and a small red minute-counter register near 12 o'clock. Two pushers at 2 and 4 o'clock operate the chronograph.

The 35mm chrome-plated case has a stainless steel snap caseback stamped Stainless Steel Back, Breveté S.G.D.G., Patented, Fond Acier Inoxydable and Fabr. Suisse, with case number. The attribution rests on the patented movement architecture and the caseback markings; the dial is unsigned and the movement is not signed with the Pierce name. It is fitted with a later black woven fabric strap.

Brand heritage

Pierce was founded in 1883 in Biel/Bienne, Switzerland, by Léon Lévy and his brothers. When the company refused to join the Ebauches SA consortium in the 1920s it had to design its own movements, and it went on to create more than 30 calibres of its own, two of them chronographs.

In the mid-1930s Léon Lévy designed the calibre 130 family as an affordable chronograph built around a central wheel, with a then-novel vertical friction clutch that was patented in 1935. The Pierce chronograph reached the market in 1936 and was produced through the 1940s.
