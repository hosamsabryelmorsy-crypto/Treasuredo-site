About this watch

A striking piece of 1970s Swiss design from Century of Biel. The rectangular matte grey case carries a raised, polished gold-tone square frame around the crystal, held by two gold-tone tabs at 12 and 6 o'clock, so the dial seems to float above the case. The anthracite grey dial is kept deliberately minimal: gold-tone baton markers with a double baton at 12, a gold "Century" signature and slim gold-tone hands. A small gold-tone knurled crown sits at 3 o'clock. Inside is a Swiss automatic (self-winding) movement; the caseback is signed "Automatic - Waterproof - Patented - Swiss" and is fixed by two screws under the strap ends. It is worn on a red crocodile-pattern leather strap and comes in its original Century Swiss presentation box.

Brand heritage

Century Time Gems was founded in Biel, Switzerland, on 5 January 1966 by Hans-Ulrich Klingenberg, on the first day the "Statut horloger" no longer barred newcomers from the Swiss watch industry. The company began as Vacuum Chronometer Corp., making vacuum-sealed watches of Klingenberg's own invention, then pioneered watch cases in ultra-hard materials: boron carbide in the late 1960s and later sapphire, for which Century is best known today. The firm is still family-run, now based in Nidau next to Biel.
