The silver dial has a striking pyramid ("clous de Paris") texture that sparkles from every angle, with the red Rado anchor logo above the RADO wordmark, "DiaStar" below centre, applied baton markers with black inlay, cream luminous dots and a framed date window at 3 o'clock. Supplied on its Rado-signed steel link bracelet and in a vintage Rado presentation box.

Brand heritage

Rado traces its roots to the Schlup & Co. watch factory founded in 1917 in Lengnau, Switzerland, and adopted the Rado name in the 1950s. In 1962 Rado launched the DiaStar, the world's first scratch-resistant watch, with a case made of hardmetal (tungsten carbide) and a sapphire crystal. The DiaStar made Rado's name as the master of hard materials and remains in the collection today.
