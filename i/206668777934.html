About this watch

The Casio CMD-10 is the famous early-1990s "Wrist Remote Controller": a digital alarm chronograph with a built-in infrared transmitter that was designed to operate TVs and VCRs from your wrist. This example carries the early module 1028 (stamped on the caseback with "CMD-10, Stainless Steel Back, Japan M") and is a Made in Japan piece. The black resin case has the red infrared window at 12, four rubber control buttons on the right (Power, TV/VCR, Stop, Play) and four arrow keys below the display, with the classic red "Wrist Remote Controller" and yellow "Alarm Chronograph" printing. It comes in a Casio acrylic display box.

Brand heritage

Casio, founded in Tokyo in 1946, launched its first watch, the Casiotron, in 1974 and became one of the great names of digital watchmaking, with gadget watches such as calculator, databank and remote-control models that are now highly collected.
