About this watch

A 1970s Rado Manhattan, the brand's bold square "TV-screen" dress watch. The black dial carries applied gold block markers, gold baton hands, a gold-framed day-date window at 3 o'clock and the signature red Rado anchor emblem beside the gold RADO logo. The gold-plated case sits under a high domed crystal and closes with a steel caseback stamped with Rado's classic twin seahorse "Water Sealed" medallion. It comes in its original gold Rado presentation box.

Brand heritage

Rado grew out of the Schlup & Co. movement workshop founded in 1917 in Lengnau, Switzerland, and began selling watches under the Rado name in the 1950s. In 1962 it launched the DiaStar, the first scratch-resistant watch. During the 1960s and 70s Rado built a range of automatic day-date watches, the Manhattan among them, with the twin seahorse caseback that collectors still look for.
