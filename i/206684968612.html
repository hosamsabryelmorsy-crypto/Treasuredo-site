About this watch

An early Seiko quartz watch in a broad, angular stainless-steel case with a stepped bezel and integrated-style bracelet, a strong piece of 1970s design and far less common than the round Seiko quartz models of the period. The silver sunburst dial is signed SEIKO QUARTZ 4004 with the SQ logo, with black baton hands, a fine centre seconds hand and an outer minute track.

The most distinctive feature is at 6 o'clock: the day and date are stacked vertically in a single framed window. The caseback is stamped with reference 4633-5029 and serial number 760587, which by Seiko's dating system points to June 1977, and it has the externally accessible battery hatch used on early Seiko quartz watches. The dial code reads 4633-5020R, matching the Seiko 4633 quartz calibre.

Brand heritage

Seiko traces its origins to 1881, when Kintaro Hattori opened a watch and jewellery shop in Tokyo; in 1892 he founded the Seikosha factory, and in 1913 it produced the Laurel, Japan's first wristwatch. In December 1969 Seiko launched the Astron, the world's first quartz wristwatch, and through the 1970s it led the move to quartz. Its early "SQ" and 4004 quartz models were built to a high standard, at a time when quartz was still a premium technology, and are now collected as part of that history.
