About this watch

This early Helvetia has the sort of dial that only decades can give a watch. The once-light surface has matured into a warm parchment-yellow tone, densely freckled with age, while the outer minute track has turned a soft salmon-beige. Against it, the large black Arabic numerals remain bold, with syringe-style hands and a long centre seconds hand.

The layout follows the military and field watches of the 1940s, but the caseback carries no military issue markings, so it is offered as a military-style civilian Helvetia. The 34mm case has a rounded bezel, gently shaped lugs and a large fluted crown; the caseback reads Waterresistant, Shockprotected, Swiss Made, Stainless Steel Back.

Inside is the signed Helvetia / General Watch Co. calibre 800C, a 17-jewel ("Seventeen Jewels", "Unadjusted") Swiss manual-wind centre-seconds movement.

Brand heritage

The Helvetia name was registered in 1880 by Louis Brandt & Frère, the firm that later became Omega. In 1895 Helvetia production passed to the new Société d'Horlogerie La Générale in La Chaux-de-Fonds, which moved to Bienne (Biel) in 1897 and also registered the English name General Watch Co., the name signed on this movement. Helvetia was one of the twelve makers of the British W.W.W. military watches of 1945, today collected as the "Dirty Dozen", and at the end of the war it introduced the centre-seconds calibre 800C used here.
