About this watch

The Edox Geoscope is one of the most distinctive world-time watches of the 1970s, and this example takes the design somewhere unexpected. In place of the usual world map, the centre of the dial carries a figurative artwork: a white-bearded figure in flowing blue robes, rendered in blue, ivory and warm coral tones with fine dark outlines and dotted shading. Around it run the black-and-white 24-hour ring, amber-toned baton hour markers and white baton hands, with the EDOX GEOSCOPE signature at the lower right.

The approximately 42mm stainless steel cushion case has the Geoscope's characteristic twin crowns and a domed crystal. It is worn on an articulated steel link bracelet with folding clasp and comes with the blue Edox presentation box shown.

Brand heritage

Edox was founded in 1884 in Biel/Bienne, Switzerland, by the watchmaker Christian Rüefli-Flury; the name comes from the ancient Greek for "measure of time". The brand is known for the Delfin of 1961, with its double caseback and double O-ring crown for water resistance.

In 1965 Victor Flury-Liechti took over the company and invested in new manufacturing technology, which led to the Geoscope of 1970: a world-time watch with a rotating 24-hour display that let the wearer read the time in every time zone. Housed in a monobloc steel compressor-style case with twin crowns, it remains one of the boldest Swiss designs of its decade.
