About this watch

This first Helvetia has the kind of dial that only decades can give a watch. The once light surface matured into a warm parchment yellow tone, densely freckled with age, while the outer minute track transformed a soft salmon beige. Against it, the large black Arabic numerals remain bold, with syringe hands and a long central seconds hand.

The layout follows military and field watches from the 1940s, but the caseback bears no signs of military emission, so it is offered as a military-style civilian Helvetia. The 34mm case has a rounded bezel, gently shaped lugs and a large grooved crown; the caseback features Waterproof, Shockproof, Swiss Made, Stainless Steel Bottom.

Inside is Helvetia / General Watch Co., a 17-jewel center-second hand-wound Swiss movement ("Seventeen Jewels", "Unadjusted").

Brand heritage

The Helvetia name was registered in 1880 by Louis Brandt & Frère, the company that later became Omega. In 1895 production of the Helvetia passed to the new Société d'Horlogerie La Générale in La Chaux-de-Fonds, which moved to Bienne (Bienne) in 1897 and also registered the English name General Watch Co., the name signed on this movement. The Helvetia was one of the twelve manufacturers of the W.W.W. British military watches from 1945, today collected as the "Dirty Dozen", and at the end of the war introduced the 800C caliber of the central seconds used here.
