About this watch

A first Seiko quartz watch in a large stainless steel corner case with stepped bezel and integrated style bracelet, a highlight of 1970s design and much less common than Seiko quartz round models of the period. The silver sunburst dial is signed SEIKO QUARTZ 4004 with the SQ logo, with black stick hands, a nice center seconds hand, and an external minute track.

The most distinctive feature is at 6 o'clock: the day and date are stacked vertically in a single framed window. The caseback is stamped with reference 4633-5029 and serial number 760587, which according to the Seiko dating system points to June 1977, and has the externally accessible battery hatch used on early Seiko quartz watches. The dial code reads 4633-5020R, corresponding to the Seiko 4633 quartz caliber.

Brand heritage

Seiko dates back to 1881, when Kintaro Hattori opened a watch and jewelry store in Tokyo; in 1892 he founded the Seikosha factory and in 1913 produced Laurel, the first Japanese wristwatch. In December 1969 Seiko launched the Astron, the world's first quartz wristwatch, and through the 1970s brought the transition to quartz. Its early "SQ" and 4004 quartz models were built to a high standard, at a time when quartz was still a premium technology, and are now collected as part of that history.
