About this watch

This early Helvetia has the type of dial that only decades can give a watch. The once light surface has matured to a warm parchment yellow tone that is densely freckled with age, while the outer minute track has turned a soft salmon beige. In contrast, the large black Arabic numerals remain bold, with syringe-style hands and a long second hand in the center.

The layout follows 1940s military and field clocks, but the caseback bears no military issue markings, so it is offered as a military-style civilian Helvetia. The 34mm case has a rounded bezel, gently shaped lugs and a large fluted crown; the caseback reads Water Resistant, Shockprotected, Swiss Made, Stainless Steel Back.

Inside is the signed Helvetia/General Watch Co. caliber 800C, a 17-jewel ("Seventeen Jewels", "Unadjusted") Swiss manual winding center seconds movement.

Brand Heritage

The name Helvetia was registered in 1880 by Louis Brandt & Frère, the company that later became Omega. In 1895 Helvetia production passed to the new Société d'Horlogerie La Générale in La Chaux-de-Fonds, which moved to Biel in 1897 and also registered the English name General Watch Co., the name signed on this movement. Helvetia was one of the twelve manufacturers of the British W.W.W. Military watches from 1945, now collected as "Dirty Dozen", and introduced the 800C center seconds caliber used here at the end of the war.
