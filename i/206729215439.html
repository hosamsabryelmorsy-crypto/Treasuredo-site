About this watch

A 1970s Seiko automatic with reference 6309-8040, powered by Seiko's own 17-jewel caliber 6309 automatic movement. The dial is a warm brown gradient, lighter in the center and darker toward the edge, with a fine pebble texture. He has applied faceted gold cane markers with white lume stripes, a double marker at 12 o'clock, and a white lume dot every hour. Gold cane hands carry white lumen inserts and a thin, dark seconds hand sweeps the dial. The day-date window at 3 o'clock has a gold frame and shows the day in Spanish (e.g. "SAB"). The cushion-shaped gold-plated case has polished wide sides, a thin round bezel, and domed crystal, and comes in a gold-plated link bracelet with a signed SEIKO drop-down clasp.

Brand Heritage

Seiko has its roots in 1881, when Kintaro Hattori opened its watch and watch store in Tokyo. Caliber 6309 was one of Seiko's automatic workhorse movements from the mid-1970s to the 1980s. It powered dress and daily date models like this as well as the famous diver "Turtle" 6309-7040, and is known for being sturdy and easy to maintain.
